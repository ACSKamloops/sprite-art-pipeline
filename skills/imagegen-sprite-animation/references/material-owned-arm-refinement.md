# Material ownership and connected arm bridges

Use this for a readable humanoid action whose material assignment or limb joins
still change between keys. Preserved pixels do not prove lively motion.

Inspect native reference pixels before naming a material. In the Hybrid study,
the reference has long brown bracers with small exposed skin hands. The first
jab instead painted a long skin shaft ending in a brown mitten. A literal
169-pixel journal repaired ownership while preserving silhouettes and clocks;
that did not fix arm mass or motion. A new closed hand is authored paint, not
recovered anatomy from a walking hand.

## Construct the actual moving part

1. Freeze the source guard and complete arm donors. Keep shoulder, sleeve,
   skin elbow, bracer, wrist and hand traceable. A fixed body is a control,
   not automatic animation quality.
2. Save native ownership polygons, separate body/arm layers and explicit
   previously occluded shirt paint. Protect unrelated head, feet and guard arm.
3. For small compatible changes, keep the hand rigid while warping the connected
   proximal arm. Save integer sample maps and reject folded triangles.
4. If a larger bend stretches shirt into a shelf, repaint that connected arm at
   the required angle. Two actual ImageGen calls supplied material keys and
   opening/recovery bridges in this study. A separate procedural forearm trial
   was rejected for its weak hand join despite successful export checks.
5. Inspect the body exposed beneath the opened arm. The hanging teal flap here
   came largely from authored underpaint. Narrowing that patch cleaned the
   contour. Fix the actual owner instead of deleting arbitrary output pixels.
6. Compare a fixed body with a declared small body contribution. One-pixel upper
   translation and a projected shoulder/chest turn are different experiments.
   Keep soles registered, head/guard hand rigid where intended, shoulder
   attachments explicit and actual source maps frozen. An authored2D turn does
   not recover hidden3D anatomy.

Layer body, occluded underpaint and moving arm separately. Initial-project
hashes, literal journals and undo/replay must follow the new source; do not force
old journals onto changed donors/maps. Whole-sheet row corrections differ from
measured shoulder attachment offsets. Neither permits full-body bbox fitting.

## Review and real tools

Keep clocks unchanged when isolating a pixel/material edit or body contribution.
Equal total duration/contact onset does not fix every other key onset. Repeated
old poses at new slot times provide an additional timing control. Reused guards
and repeated exposures are not new drawings.

Inspect every pair, entry and recovery at exact holds. Separate fidelity,
drawing, chronology, continuous human playback and gameplay. If a numeric
judgment is holistic and has no actionable defect, record that rather than
inventing a repair or rerating identical art to obtain a desired number.
The initial study review preferred its slight-lean painted-part jab, but did
not establish the requested production threshold or library acceptance.

The three-layer, ten-exposure study was actually saved/reopened by LibreSprite
batch mode. Native cels and the editor-rendered sheet matched. Browser48Hz tick
samples and a Godot source/input/state experiment also passed. These prove
transport/state behavior, not anatomy or normal-speed feel.

## Persistent head and planted support components

Generated combat keys changed the approved walking face: wider head, larger eyes
and a different expression. Compare the complete native head against the actual
approved source before diagnosing this as a palette problem. The follow-on test
reuses the literal original SE head, including dark eye/outline pixels, over the
new body. A semantic skin/hair-only extraction first lost those dark pixels;
retain the complete owned head instead. This is one known view, not a new angle.

Declare the old head/upper-neck removal mask separately from the new head owner.
Protect the adjacent raised fist and scarf. Save the head attachment track and
dense source coordinates for every output pixel, including transparent erasures.
The local trial uses an explicit one-pixel horizontal track, fixed vertical
attachment and two editable layers; it preserves the existing body below row31.
That boundary is fixture-specific, not a universal humanoid anatomy rule.
Keep upstream authoring layers when choosing a delivery: body, hidden shirt,
arm and replacement head remain four separate editable layers in the final
authoring control. Flattening them for a head-only experiment does not remove
the need to retain the original part ownership. Verify that the richer layered
file renders the exact same complete RGBA before transferring its existing
review. This is authoring preservation, not new art or a higher quality score.

The full-body trial also moved its rear boot footprint by four pixels despite
keeping the sole height. Check contact spans/landmarks as well as bounding-box
bottoms. A second control reuses the entire frame00 lower component below row52,
including its transparent pixels, across all eight keys. This actually fixes
the soles, but can introduce a hip/hem seam or excess stiffness. Inspect the
whole join and connected legs at every exposure. A planted compact jab can use
this component; a step, kick, dodge or walking action needs a different support
contract. Copying one body component is not proof of realistic foot physics.

Keep feet-only, head-plus-feet and painted-part-body controls separate. The
preserved walking head has prior walking acceptance only. Neither it nor a
successful source-map replay transfers acceptance to the new combat animation.
The local replay witnesses and tests are under `persistent-lower-body-v1/`,
`source-head-planted-full-v1/` and `source-head-planted-parts-v1/` in the study.

## Portable offline example

`assets/part-jab-study/` retains the initial slight-lean candidate, source layers,
literal journal, expected frames and actual editor artifacts. This unapproved
study precedes the narrower-underpaint/torso-turn alternatives. It is not an
accepted character library or arbitrary rig. See its `PROVENANCE.json`.

With Pillow and the sibling construction toolkit available, run:

```bash
python3 scripts/verify_part_jab_fixture.py
```

The verifier writes nothing and invokes no model/editor. It replays the layered
journal/undo/redo, source pins, ASE project, retained native cels/tool sheet and
all ten RGBA outputs. The full local study, failed trials, raw returns, prompts,
maps and later reviews are `/home/astra/artifacts/hero-rpg-quality-20261021/`.
