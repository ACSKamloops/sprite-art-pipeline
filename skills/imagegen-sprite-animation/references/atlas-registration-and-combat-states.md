# Multi-action atlas registration and combat states

Use this when expanding an accepted actor into directional action banks. Freeze
the preferred source and list coverage by direction/action before generating.
One-view acceptance does not transfer to the new bank.

Keep the target view explicit in the actual input pixels. A full turnaround or
strong opposite-facing walk can overwhelm a written direction instruction.
Crop the relevant view as a reference and separate pose, appearance and camera
authority. Inspect every action row: a correct idle does not prove a correct
punch or dodge facing. Mirrored references need their lighting/asymmetry limits
recorded; they are not automatically production banks.

Do not assume the generated atlas obeys the requested equal cell boundaries.
Inspect whole-sheet alpha and actual complete bodies before slicing. A boot
spilling over a nominal line can appear as a floating bar in the next row.
Shared row/column partitions can be moved into measured empty gutters while
keeping the original source-coordinate origins. Save the raw source and exact
ownership boundaries; do not delete unexplained fragments to make a test green.

Distinguish atlas layout drift from intended action motion. A large repeated
column ramp across unrelated walk, punch, cast and idle rows is evidence for a
common column correction. Author and freeze that group correction once; do not
fit every head, foot or silhouette independently. With scale s, a desired integer
stage translation t uses a source-sampling offset -t/s. Exact rational arithmetic
and frozen maps avoid resampling-phase drift. Verify the new RGBA against an
integer-translated old-raster control, including alpha, all pairs and row/wrap
seams. Registration does not supply missing limbs, a real rig or better gait roles.

Make action states concrete. A raised combat guard, a spell-gather pose and an
arms-down neutral can all be valid but different. Bind completion to an actual
painted pose and test the controller's selected pixels. Reusing a source guard
at an endpoint is deliberate pose selection, not a new recovery drawing.
Stable static idle is an honest fallback when the purported breathing alternate
rocks sideways; report its one unique drawing rather than claiming an animated
breath. Do not disguise missing extension/recovery with extra repeated slots.

Check the acting shoulder/arm through every attack and spell pose. If a wrong-hand
pose is excluded, document the simpler selected motion and remaining bridge gap.
An exact final idle can remove a state-boundary snap while moving the same
problem inside the action: inspect the preceding recovery pose too. A seven-pixel
support change is not solved by an equal final-state hash. A deliberately simpler
duck can reuse its valid preparation in reverse when the generated recovery has
the wrong leg scale. Declare the reused drawing and simple motion; do not call it
a newly painted recovery bridge, roll or universally appropriate dodge.

Also compare the action row's support plane to its actual idle control. In the
tested SE duck, reversing preparation did not solve a common action-row origin
error. A declared +9Y translation of the entire selected crouch source restored
the boot row to 75 through all four exposures without clipping or losing paint;
S needed +2Y and retained one pixel of variation. Save the unshifted control and
prove every crouch exposure equals the same integer translation. This fixes a
registration defect only: the SE head still changes 14 pixels into/out of the
crouch, requiring genuine new bridge paint for smoother lowering/recovery.

Keep image editing, selection, timing, export and engine proofs separate.
Programmatic source selection needs manifest/frame pins, actual RGBA equality,
positive rational holds, unique-raster counts and an endpoint relation. New paint
needs its real request and raw return. Native editor, APNG, browser and Godot
readback confirm transport/state behavior, not drawing quality or human playback.
