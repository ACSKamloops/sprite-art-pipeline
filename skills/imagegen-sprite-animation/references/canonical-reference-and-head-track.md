# Canonical appearance and a source-owned head track

Use this when newly painted action keys have useful limb mechanics but change
face, head size or costume as they join existing art. This is a tested authoring
route with unresolved art quality, not a general guarantee of consistency.

## Freeze the right authority

Required inputs are the actual accepted appearance clip, its native stage/pivot,
the selected neutral, ordered action keys with rational holds, and explicit
appearance-versus-motion reference roles. Inspect them together at their literal
native coordinates. A neutral already used by the engine may still be an
unapproved or differently proportioned drawing.

The local SE study found a broader, offset idle compared with the preferred walk.
Forcing get-up into that idle had introduced a late volume change. A separate
neutral combined the accepted walk's complete upper with planted source legs.
It was reviewed against that walk before being used as the new endpoint. Its
copied hurt/landing endpoints were refreshed together; source identity alone
does not certify those action boundaries.

Do not normalize each figure by its bounding box to make the comparison pass.
Head contour, facial features, belt/shoulder landmarks, costume volumes and
projected support feet provide distinct evidence. Pose and camera can explain
some width differences, so a pigment envelope is only a measurement proxy.

## Generate the missing mechanics with corrected references

Give ImageGen the corrected canonical appearance as the appearance authority.
An earlier action strip may guide knee, hip and hand progression while its face
and rendering remain unsuitable. State those roles in the actual saved request.
Ask for a small related-key group that explains a demonstrated gap, rather than
regenerating the entire bank to hide one transition.

One two-key rising-pose call supplied a bent-knee push-off and an almost-standing
pose after earlier painting had inherited the wrong neutral. The requested
1280×640 sheet actually returned1774×887. The import retained that raw output,
used one common80/887 scale and a declared shared stage translation(-3,0), and
recorded nearest source coordinates and binary-alpha preparation. These numbers
belong to this case, not a template for future returns. No per-pose fitting ran.

The new keys improved knee/hip staging but their heads were still small and
slightly different. Painting success established new candidate anatomy, not a
finished action. Keep the raw painted route beside the component hybrid.

## Attach accepted components without erasing good motion

Trace head/face/scarf ownership explicitly in both the accepted donor and the
target. Clear the old owned paint, including unwanted silhouette pixels, then
place the complete source part at an authored neck attachment. Keep target
cuffs, tunic, hips and boots protected unless they require a separate repair.
Save source IDs, inverse integer coordinates, masks and the final visible diff.

The hybrid retained newly painted bodies while using one accepted head/scarf at
two different rigid translations. That kept the same head size and camera while
the leg pose changed. Source pixels can move as an attached part; this differs
from independently fitting the entire frame. Do not enlarge a whole character
to disguise a head mismatch.

Inspect component boundaries at native size. A red-scarf classifier initially
also cleared three skin pixels from a nearby cuff. An outside-part pixel guard
caught this; the final ownership repair restored the original arm pixels. Broad
color predicates are not semantic owners. Source palette overlap, transparent
gaps and nearby sleeves need spatial ownership and actual changed-pixel checks.

A separate whole-cuff substitution left two detached old-cuff fragments and
shortened the reaching gesture. It was rejected rather than credited because
its exports replayed. Connected-component checks can expose debris; connected
paint alone cannot prove an elbow, sleeve or grasp is correct.

## Review the whole component track

Repairing only the last two faces can move the model-change cut earlier. The
next trial therefore reused the canonical head/scarf across compatible seated
and braced poses as well. It preserved the original body/limb movement and
clock, with separate authored attachments per pose and no head resizing.

This is limited to compatible views. A rigid upright/rightward face can become
an inappropriate gaze on a deeply folded torso. Inspect neck attachment, camera,
scarf depth, shoulder volume and the first join from any retained old head. If
the pose needs a new view or head tilt, paint that view or author a supported
rotation; a flat donor does not recover unseen anatomy. All canonical part
pixels need not remain visible when another body part correctly occludes them.

When a raised knee passes in front of the scarf, use a separately traced
foreground knee owner and explicit layer order. Do not infer correct depth from
the fact that the head/scarf paste was exact. Keep both the unobscured source
part and the actual visible composite, with the occlusion declared.

Compare the full action and every adjacent pair at native and nearest enlargement
on contrasting backgrounds. Keep the actual neutral after completion. Review
new mechanics, temporal identity and practical use separately: the anonymous
round3 reviewer preferred the hybrid narrowly, while retaining a visible join
from the older head and no9/10 approval. Equal leg pixels received equal gait
ratings even when the head treatment differed.

If a release cleanup occurs after a review packet is frozen, retain the original
report. Supply the exact changed-pixel delta and affected pair for a limited
addendum. The three-pixel cuff cleanup was confirmed without rewriting the
earlier scores or silently transferring approval to changed inputs.

## Author missing underpaint deliberately

The source-part bridge trial exposed diagonal collar, sleeve and waist gaps.
The pure-warp result was retained as rejected. A second study added an explicit
new pencil layer with sampled source material colors:20 pixels on one bridge
and28 on the other. The first20-pixel stage of the latter still left a visible
connection gap and was retained separately.

These are newly authored cloth/outline pixels, not recovered invisible anatomy
or literal donor texels. Freeze the intended seam owner and individual pencil
coordinates; do not flood every transparent hole. The tested layer kept all
existing opaque paint and all ten original key drawings unchanged. Guarded
history replay/undo and editor/media export checks verified the operation;
geometry and timed quality still require their own review.

When adding a bridge, preserve original key onsets and total duration. This
study split two0.1s holds into0.05s source plus0.05s bridge, then compared the
new drawings against repeated source poses in the same twelve slots. A clean
neck seam or an additional drawing does not by itself prove better weight
transfer. Prefer upstream hidden-part paint for a reusable rig; an explicit
output-space patch can be honest for a bounded two-cel repair.

## Keep world and native units consistent

Read actual runtime movement and sprite scale before judging a timing control.
The local inspector used100 world units/s for walking,160 for running, and3×
sprite scale. Reusing the one-second preferred walk at the actual8/5 speed ratio
therefore gives a5/8-second cycle. It creates no flight pose or new run drawing.

A native1× viewer must move that actor at160/3 native pixels/s, not160. Likewise,
for a painted source point `p`, pivot `q`, world root `r(t)` and scale `s`, use
`world_point(t) = r(t) + s * (p - q)`. Adding world displacement directly to native
shoe coordinates mixes units. The first diagnostic made that mistake; the
incorrect viewer/plot were preserved and superseded with a correction receipt.

Tracked visible shoe pixels are not automatically the same material point or
physical stance contact across drawings. The corrected plot remains a diagnostic
of explicit source picks and a modeled root, not a no-slip or runtime-contact
certificate. Retiming an accepted walk can supply a useful fast-walk control;
it must not be mislabeled as a separately authored run.

## Check support changes before adding more exposures

For a hand-supported rise, trace the actual shoulder, elbow, wrist, pelvis and
both visible boot owners in the neighboring drawings. Use native coordinates
with an uncertainty estimate. A request such as "lift the rear knee" is not a
measurement: the local source actually moved the rear sole downward in the
projected image as the stance opened. The first proposed upward-leg repair was
abandoned after inspecting the real boot owner, excluding the nearby hand.

Separate three possible deficits: missing arm articulation, missing connected
body/leg paint, and an abrupt whole-body support change. An arm-only bridge can
solve the first while leaving the others intact. The tested two-segment source
arm kept its complete cuff/hand rigid, used an authored shoulder/elbow chain,
and added27 explicit tunic pixels where moving the arm exposed hidden cloth.
A companion rigid rear-boot move broke the leg/body connection and was rejected.
Neither successful source replay nor one connected silhouette proves useful
weight transfer.

Ask for new painted anatomy when the donor genuinely lacks it. Inspect the
returned pose against the intended phase before making head or palette repairs.
One single-pose call produced connected crouching anatomy but placed the support
hand nearly at the next key, skipping the requested midpoint. Its rear sole was
near the desired intermediate height; its hand was not. It was rejected as the
requested intermediate even after a literal canonical head corrected identity.
The prompt's coordinates are an instruction, not evidence the model obeyed them.

A bounded hybrid may reuse the new anatomy while articulating the failed arm,
but that is another candidate with its own owner masks, seam paint and review.
Do not describe it as an unchanged generated result or a recovered hidden rig.
In the actual anonymous four-way test, the shorter original painted arm was
preferred to the coordinate-corrected arm: the latter looked stretched and
merged with the rear-leg silhouette. The original failed exact midpoint
compliance yet gave the best local body/arm balance of those trials. Keep those
two findings separate. Do not extend a hand toward a target coordinate after
the torso has already changed its support geometry.
Compare complete action progression, not just the repaired three-pose crop.
If the main reorganization remains elsewhere, revise the action's key plan
instead of indefinitely adding small bridges around it.

For a fair insertion test, freeze source poses and their rational onsets. Split
the earlier hold into source-plus-bridge and give the repeated-source control
the identical split. The local release study inserted at.475s by splitting
the.45s key's.05s hold into.025s+.025s; the next old key remained at.5s and the
action still ended at.8s. These are case timings, not a universal recovery pace.

## Audit shared neutral contracts without spreading one repair

Before replacing neutrals in other directions, establish each direction's
appearance authority. The local seven-direction audit found no clear repeat
of the SE enlarged/offset idle fault. E placement, SW view and NW gait anchors
needed review; their unapproved walks were not automatically better targets.
No other-direction art changed on the strength of those observations.

Inventory literal first/last endpoint equality separately from art similarity.
Fifteen other-direction selected endpoints exactly matched an idle exposure:
hurt and landing in all seven directions, plus S dash. That identifies coupled
contracts to revisit if the corresponding idle changes; it neither proves
historical copy provenance nor certifies the animation between endpoints.
