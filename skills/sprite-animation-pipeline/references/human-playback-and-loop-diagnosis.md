# Human playback and loop diagnosis

Use this after a user watches a timed comparison. Record the exact candidate hashes, original feedback, selected variant, scope and any requested changes. A human preference about cadence should guide the next artistic experiment when automated reviewers only reconstructed motion from stills. Keep technical defects, subjective preferences, baseline acceptance and numeric review targets separate.

When a loop appears to freeze:

1. Inspect actual sample timestamps and durations across every internal cycle seam. Check the final media-file restart separately: browser buffering or seeking can differ from an internal animation boundary.
2. Compare the actual last poses, first poses and their exposures. Several different pictures can hold a similar gesture. Exact unique-frame counts and regular timestamps cannot disprove a perceived pause.
3. Compare the original motion source and the phases retained for painted key poses. Sparse selection, a redraw and uniform exposure are different interventions. Do not attribute a pause to one without a controlled comparison.
4. If shortening a plateau changes total cycle duration, include a uniform-speed control with that same new duration. Preserve the PNG bytes and declare every new hold. Otherwise faster playback can masquerade as better spacing.
5. Verify all decoded lossless pixels and actual media timestamps. Inspect the wrap at native scale. Let human playback settle cadence; do not promote an unviewed diagnostic.

For part editing, a hand emerging from occlusion can belong to the other arm. Follow the shoulder/elbow chain and visible source paint; do not track whichever glove is more exposed. A concentrated but coherent arc is not automatically a defect. Likewise, ambiguous near/far labels across a loop are hypotheses, not authority to force an implausible limb crossing.

Once the user approves a baseline, preserve it unchanged. Put experiments in separate outputs and make promotion explicit. Do not redraw accepted motion merely to seek a higher reviewer score. A single accepted walk does not certify other actions, views, transitions or physical foot contact.
