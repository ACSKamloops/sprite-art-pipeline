# Visual quality must block delivery

The Copper Scout V2 case passed exact exports, clocks, IK and measured contact while the user rejected the entire drawing and motion quality. Its previous prototype visual acceptance was too permissive. Treat it as a rejected visual baseline, preserving its true mechanical proofs. Review completeness and hash freshness establish what was inspected; they cannot establish that the inspection was good.

Before extending an animation library, approve a small representative art and motion pilot at the intended native display size. Include a coherent front/back/side turnaround and one complete walk cycle; do not use a side drawing with limb coordinates rearranged as proof of convincing depth. Establish a concrete visual benchmark and character proportions, clothing construction, light direction and anatomical near/far equipment ownership. Missing views remain missing.

Block visual approval for weak or inconsistent anatomical volume, mechanical limb motion, unreadable weight transfer, incorrect gait phase, changing garment/boot masses, disconnected joints, or attacks that point along the screen when the character should aim into depth. A deliberately exaggerated style is valid when its design and motion are intentional and coherent. Draw contact, compression, passing and push-off keys explicitly; inspect opposing arm/leg phases and overlap, not only limb endpoints. Run must differ in mechanics and silhouette, rather than being a faster walk.

Compare each adjacent pose, wrap and supported transition, then compare equivalent phases across the two half cycles and direction views. Ask what the character's mass, bones, garment and equipment would do in those poses. Separate intentional bob, squash, occlusion and cloth follow-through from accidental redrawing. No automatic metric can make that judgment alone. Review full sprites, silhouette-only displays, material regions and actual timed playback at native size. Scores and source hashes supplement visual judgment.

Separate frame registration from anatomy repair. A known common sheet-row offset may be corrected with an explicit shared layout transform; keep raw and calibrated versions visible. Per-frame fitting/recentering can conceal a bad gait and destroy the intended motion. A global offset cannot repair changed limb length, garment silhouette or an incorrect opposite-phase arm. Rebuild measured world contacts after any art correction.

AI-generated pose sheets are candidates. In the follow-up experiment, richer pixel-style drawing initially repeated nearly identical wide strides. An explicit pose guide produced passing knees, but the result still shifted the ground by 5–6 native pixels between halves, changed scarf/boot shapes, and duplicated opposing-phase arm configurations. Both candidates were rejected. Stronger illustration is not a finished walk cycle. Use generation for design and proposed key poses; preserve an editable authoritative character source and deliberately repair the keys. Merely slicing and playing a generated sheet does not demonstrate a repeatable high-quality animation route.

Independent reviewers must state concrete blocking defects and the intended visual benchmark. Do not call assets visually acceptable merely because they are useful for testing an engine. Record mechanical acceptance, visual rejection/acceptance and user feedback separately. User rejection supersedes current visual acceptance; keep the earlier receipts unchanged as history, update every current handoff/viewer status, and do not inherit a failed aesthetic judgment through pixel equality. New source-bound inspection must assess the repaired defect and overall visual quality, with candid coverage and limits.

For detailed humanoids, distinguish a source-owned cutout, a pose-specific redraw and a genuinely projected volume. A cutout can keep every ornament stable yet move like a hinged board. A redraw can produce attractive armour depth yet move or enlarge the planted boot. The upright Captain controls demonstrated both failures. Require coherent phase geometry, support, proportions and shell perspective together; preserving one does not excuse failure of the others.

Reviewers should receive the intended style, source-bound candidate, concrete movement reference and inspection requirements without a target rating. Request a candid verdict and strongest observable repair before any score. Record score uncertainty and actual inspected media. If the interface only exposes decoded images, describe chronological frame coverage and clock verification; never label that continuous real-time video viewing. Provide the actual normal-speed video for the user and keep their visual acceptance separate.

Calibrate the criticism against the camera and an actual geometric control. In the later unarmed hero case, compact-versus-wide opposing strides initially looked like inconsistent limb construction at 96 px. Native 768 px inspection and a camera-matched real 3D walk showed that substantial projected spacing asymmetry and complete boot occlusion could be valid. The reviewer qualified the categorical anatomy criticism while retaining concerns about restrained body/arm performance. Preserve both reports and their source scopes. Do not mirror a half-cycle, force equal screen-space lengths or invent a background gap merely to satisfy an unsupported review rule. A valid 3D control also does not certify that a generated output follows it or has appealing timing, surface continuity or physical contact.

Keep full-stage decoded sheets for camera/root evidence, and additional fixed stage-coordinate crops at useful native/nearest2x size for art detail. Use the same crop throughout a variant; do not fit or recenter each pose. Exact video/PNG agreement validates presentation, not convincing motion.

## Contact checks must survive deliberately displaced material

Projected ground vertices covered by opaque pixels are insufficient proof
of planting. In an independent Captain test, a boot shifted downward10px
still covered its old contact vertices and passed proximity; actual raster
comparison rejected the displaced boot. Keep this negative control. Check
material identity and ground-facing boundary/surface against the declared
world transform, not any opaque interior pixel near a planned marker.

Separate continuous model contacts, baked in-place PNG poses, held-frame
world consumption and final video placement. A correct mesh equation does
not certify all four. Record tolerances in native pixels, boundary sampling,
contacts hidden by perspective, and unsupported claims explicitly. Physical
translation previews need a fixed stage that contains the entire chosen
number of loops at both zooms, or an explicitly declared following camera.
Clipping is a presentation defect to repair before the actual motion review.

## Calibrate criticism before editing correct motion

Preserve the blind verdict, then test its strongest concrete claim. Unequal displacement at equal holds is expected near motion extrema. In the retained hero case, two alleged spacing faults coincided with the guide's slow reversal/settle; the reviewer qualified them as playback hypotheses, not demonstrated guide mismatches. Do not linearize valid easing merely to equalize adjacent pixel differences. This does not certify every generated in-between.

Compare complete parts before and after occlusion. A far glove that looked smaller during passing retained 51–56 projected pixels before occlusion; only 18 and 4 were visible in two passing poses. That did not justify shrinking the exposed glove. Source consistency does not prove attractive anatomy, but can disprove a proposed cause.
