# Independent Captain art gate

The approved Captain image sets the identity and pixel-craft target. Approval requires a useful moving sprite, not an impressive source image with weaker replacement legs. Engineering results and effort earn no art points.

Use the shared weights for comparable reporting: art match 20%, anatomy 15%, joints 15%, pixel craft 10%, cyclic weight 20%, gear attachment 10%, game usability 10%. I score these independently. Overall must reach 9/10, every category at least 8.5, and there must be no critical identity, joint-gap, grip, clipping, sole-contact or loop failure. An untested required gate remains open rather than being assumed passed.

| Category | Independent criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Art match | Same Captain, armour mass, face, red/gold material hierarchy and approved three-quarter orientation throughout. |
| Anatomy | Plausible hip–knee–ankle chain inside the armour; consistent limb length; clear near/far depth and weight-bearing leg. |
| Joints | Hip and knee volumes remain joined under motion; armour plates overlap intentionally; no exposed holes or crushed hinges. |
| Pixel craft | Deliberate clusters, clean contour rhythm, stable highlights and trim; replacement parts match the source's detail density and perspective. |
| Cyclic weight | Contact/down/passing/up phases, alternate support, coherent pelvis transfer and swing clearance; wrap is a normal adjacent transition, not a reset. |
| Gear attachment | Sword, shield, pistol and ornaments remain attached to believable grips/straps; occlusion and motion follow the carrier. |
| Game usability | Native-size action is legible; fixed origin and silhouettes hold; timed playback and moving-world stance work at ordinary speed. |

Final review must include every frame at native size and nearest enlargement, all adjacent pairs including wrap, actual native timed player captures, and moving-world gameplay with anatomical sole observations. Decorative ankle targets and declared stance flags do not prove contact. A flat sole sample must be tied to the same rendered image and physical translation. Single frame hashes cannot establish quality.

*Motion and gameplay scores are provisional warning estimates from the two displayed poses, not a completed timed evaluation.*

The torso, face, sword and shield preserve the approved identity well. The replacement lower body does not preserve its quality. The near-side foot is stretched outward and flatter, the shin tapers toward a compressed knee, and the wide lateral spread reads as a squat/splayed stance rather than a heavy forward march. The approved pose has much stronger continuous armoured thigh–knee–shin mass. In the assembled version the pelvis/leg depth is harder to follow behind the hanging pistol and shield; those objects conceal the problem rather than resolve it.

Frames 00 and 06 keep nearly the same wide boot arrangement despite being half a cycle apart. From these two poses there is no convincing opposite-leg support exchange or narrow passing silhouette. That is a priority design problem; more bobbing, cloth variation or perfect playback cannot turn the stance into a credible walk. The gold trim and red highlight clusters on the new lower leg also look less volumetric and less coherent than the untouched upper armour.

Repair priorities: design the two opposite contact poses and two passing poses as complete anatomical chains first; restore large armoured knee/thigh volumes and narrower passing clearance; establish readable depth under the pelvis; then align material clusters and attachment occlusion. Inspect full-body native poses throughout, not only isolated legs. Keep the approved upper-body identity, but allow physically motivated pelvis/torso response rather than keeping a battle-pose torso rigid over moving feet.

No obvious detached sword/shield grip appears in these two images, but all-frame attachment has not been tested. No contact, loop or full-animation approval is granted. I am ready to review a frozen revised full sequence independently.
