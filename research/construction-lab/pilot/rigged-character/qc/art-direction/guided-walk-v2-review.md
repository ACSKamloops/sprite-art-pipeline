## Improvements worth keeping

The hood, tunic, gloves and boots have solid volumes rather than the previous rig's narrow contour rods. Contact/down/passing/up silhouettes are more varied; the passing poses visibly gather the legs. Scarf and costume make a readable original character at the audition size. Those are useful design advances, not an approval.

## Blocking findings

1. **Opposite UP phase is not convincingly authored.** Pose 8 repeats pose 4's visible arm-back/forward-boot arrangement. The colored guide reverses the red/blue arm relationship between those poses. Pose 5 does switch the visible arm swing from pose 1, but that opposition is lost by pose 8. Near/far leg identity is not consistently explained by occlusion and shading. Require an explicit A/B limb overlay through all eight poses; do not infer correct alternation from the labels or from eight distinct files.
2. **Second half has a different ground placement.** Cell-relative opaque bottoms are 478/481/481/478 in row 1 and 458/458/458/453 in row 2 (exclusive bounds). At the declared 0.24 scale, the paired shift is approximately 5–6 native pixels. A loop would jump at 4→5 and 8→1. This is sheet placement drift, not the intended down/up body bob. A declared translation can audition it, but stance versus swing anchors must first be identified: an airborne boot is not a ground anchor.
3. **Placement correction does not solve shape drift.** Opposite contact/down/up poses change calf/boot, coat and hood shape instead of maintaining the same character masses. These changes are subtle in the detailed master but visible in the native audition. Use an unchanged turnaround model and overlay paired phases after the *same disclosed* anchor correction; reject inconsistent leg length or garment volume rather than rescaling each pose to fit.
4. **Secondary cloth breaks continuity.** The passing pose 7 has a short dark vertical scarf tail where passing pose 3 retains a long cream diagonal tail. Clothing may swing and occlude, but this change has no established continuity across the neighboring frames and reads as a different drawing. The adjacent sequence must explain the tail's travel; hiding it abruptly is not follow-through.
5. **Pixel language is not yet approved.** Large-source shading and contour texture create a polished illustration, but nearest reduction leaves fine speckled edge/shading details. The exported head and scarf read; the smaller cuffs, face and boot-edge clusters need native-size cleanup and consistent material shading. A high-resolution illustration being reduced without blur is not itself proof of deliberate pixel-art clusters.
