# Independent Captain v7 sprite art / humanoid motion review

**Verdict: reject as finished, detailed humanoid game art.** This is a recognizable and useful prerendered animation prototype. It does not yet achieve the intended upright master's character presentation or convincing, polished combat-carry walk. Native384 improves detail clarity; it does not remove the principal visual weaknesses. This verdict comes from the images, not rig checks, collision counts, export reproducibility or a requested score threshold.

Indicative ratings on a ten-point finished-game-art scale: **craft 6.5 ±0.7; motion 6.0 ±1.0; overall fitness for this target 6.0 ±0.8.** Uncertainty is an estimate of review confidence, not a statistical interval. Motion is a diagnostic/provisional rating because I can inspect discrete images but did not ingest continuous real-time video. Overall is a holistic production judgment, not an arithmetic average with engineering tests. I did not use earlier scores as a baseline. A deliberately miniature-like game could accept a different aesthetic; the supplied detailed upright character is the target here.

## Whole figure and gait, before ornament

The opposite halves genuinely differ: skull-knee/screen-left leg recovers through roughly000–011 then reaches forward around013–018; the flame-knee/screen-right leg recovers around024–035 and reaches forward around046–051. By008–011 and039–043 the feet become more nearly alongside one another. Side001/117 show a bent rear leg and relatively extended support leg; side033/099 show the opposite lead and heel/sole presentation. This is not a duplicated same-leg key sequence, and it has appreciably more anatomical progression than a flat cutout with sliding feet.

However, in the delivered quarter view the enormous shin/ankle shells, short exposed thigh, tabard and shield reduce the readability of the pelvis and supported thigh. The small tucked head, wide shoulders, chest and carried props remain a very similar compact mass while the lower legs exchange. The visual result is a marching miniature: the leg action is legible, but the shoulder/pelvis relationship communicates less muscular support, release and intent than the detailed game-character target requires. Around008–011/039–043 there is little visible opening or rising of the torso; around016/049 the forward boot presents strongly while the upper mass remains almost the same carry picture. I can see a small overall bob and torso change; I cannot read a strong, differentiated loaded/down versus release/up relationship in either half. This is an image-based judgment, not an assertion about center of mass, joint forces or an absent authored channel.

A raised sword and front shield reasonably suppress free arm swing. Both remain readable and appear attached in all inspected frames; a generic unarmed swinging arm would be the wrong repair. Nevertheless, props keeping their grip does not make the pose feel alert. The shield obscures the same large torso area throughout. The weapon hand's small changes and cape lag do not create enough complementary chest/hip/carry movement to sell the character's full weight and readiness.

Actual world-key crops preserve a coherent ground reference and show travel rather than an isolated floating cutout. There is no obvious catastrophic foot teleport or limb identity swap in the inspected representatives. That does **not** prove stationary raster foot contact within the held cel; the120Hz continuous mode explicitly has sub-cel root positions, and sparse key crops cannot certify its foot planting. No fine contact pass is issued here. The source pose64→0 wrap looks small, consistent with the nearby beginning/end recovery positions; I do not see a major wrap snap. The separate sourceendpoint is not exactly the first image. No cross-action transitions were supplied/reviewed.

## Craft, material and source design

Strengths: sword/shield/halo identify the character immediately; flame and skull knees stay on their correct leg; red shells have real curved form; the shield skull, scrolls and border survive native384 and remain surprisingly readable at256. The colour grouping gives a strong focal hierarchy, and there is no conspicuous random texture replacement between adjacent poses. Sword length, halo, shoulder ornaments and shield design remain stable enough to avoid a redraw-flutter look. The face is more readable at384 than256.

The render still has uneven production finish. At256 the face compresses into a bright crown and dark brow/eye mask; at3842x it has more planes but still a pale cap-like top and tightly shadowed expression. The master's exposed head and distinct ocular detail have a clearer personality. The downward/tucked head and compact chest posture change its upright, commanding design into a hunched miniature presentation. This is a source-fidelity issue as well as appeal; preservation of colours and ornaments does not compensate for changed expression and stance.

Broad layered ankle collars and thick black sole/cap surfaces are conspicuous at013–018 and046–051. They make the leading boot look blunt and heavily segmented. Side033/099 expose rather cylindrical, sparsely articulated red shin/thigh masses and broad flat dark boot faces, while the quarter front carries much richer ornament. The limb material therefore appears less deliberately sculpted and finished when it rotates. Black joint material is reasonable, but its visible width and relation to red shell edges need visual design, not a technical zero-change requirement.

Back001/033/099/117 has a large, pale, near-trapezoidal cape with regular smooth folds and little localized wear/edge construction, together with conspicuously simple backgear. It reads as a generic cloth panel beside the densely decorated front. Since no true source back was supplied, I cannot call its invented back design inaccurate; it is a directional finish mismatch. The source's pixel-cluster edges, small gold trim and face details are crisper/more intentional than the soft prerendered material gradients and bright gold patches here. Native384 offers more samples but the same decisions.

## Top three bounded repairs before expanding the library

1. **Restore upright character presentation.** Compare000/008/016/041/049 directly against the supplied master: the tucked small head/compact shoulder-chest arrangement dominates every frame. Author one neutral and both loaded/release extremes with a less downward head presentation and readable neck/chest separation; preserve the sword/shield carry. Review those five renders against the master before rebaking65 frames. Include a face-material pass on the bright crown/eye implant region. This needs pose/design approval, not independent rescaling of every cel.
2. **Finish ankle and rotating shell construction.** Start with013/016/018 and046/049/051 plus side033/099. Narrow/shape the collar-to-boot transition and make the dark exposed sole/cap edges feel like designed armour/sole construction rather than broad stacked rings. Refine the side shin/thigh silhouette/planes to the front's finish level. Keep the existing anatomical leg ownership and test both extreme forward feet; do not hide the problem with a screen mask or a static boot pasted over motion.
3. **Make support/release readable through the held carry.** Start with008→016→024 and041→049→057, inspect silhouette first. Author a modest, coherent pelvis/chest relationship and differentiated absorption/release while the gripping arms remain constrained. Aim for an alert, balanced armored stride, not exaggerated stamping or unarmed arm swing. Rebake only after the key sequence sells its mass, then recheck all65 adjacencies and actual world held-foot material. Native384 or additional intermediate frames alone cannot perform this repair.

These repairs are proposed bounds, not authorization to change the candidate or a promise of a higher score. Final suitability still needs fresh whole-cycle inspection and direct human normal-speed viewing. The frozen candidate remains unapproved.
