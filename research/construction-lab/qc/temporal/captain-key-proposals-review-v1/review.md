Both proposals are rejected as usable Captain walks. They are useful whole-body pose experiments, not approved animation keys. The upright version improves the standing drawing, but does not solve the support progression.

This review uses decoded frames from the delivered comparison MP4s, not source re-rendering. All eight held keys were inspected at native size and nearest 2x, all chronological key boundaries and the 7→0 wrap, plus native silhouette keys and selected half-speed decoded keys. This is ordered-frame inspection with verified timestamps, **not continuous real-time video ingestion**. The structural defects below are visible without claiming a complete real-time impression.

Old-source proposal:

- Keys 0→1→2 (0–450ms) largely preserve the screen-left gold-kneecap leg below the trunk while changing the other boot. Keys 4→5 repeat a similar configuration rather than clearly presenting the reverse support exchange. Key 6 (900–1050ms) suddenly exposes a large forward-facing dark boot sole and red-kneecap leg; it reads as a kick/camera change, not a leg passing under a coherent pelvis. The ownership and occlusion of the two decorated legs cannot be followed reliably.
- The shield skull, lower ornament, gold border, tabard and belt attachments change drawing across the cycle. The shield's shape/detail change is especially apparent between keys 2, 4 and 6. They are not stable objects simply carried through a pose.
- The second half-cycle raises and reshapes the entire kit. Source alpha bottoms vary from 244/245/248/244 in keys 0–3 to 234/238/238/239 in keys 4–7. These bounds are not a measured support landmark; nevertheless, the decoded fixed-grid sequence visibly shifts the body and costume between the rows. Key 7→0 restores a different silhouette and larger hanging kit.

Upright proposal:

- Keys 1/2 and 5/6 again show the screen-left gold-kneecap leg extended below the trunk and the screen-right red-kneecap boot lifting. The intended opposite half is not demonstrated by those poses. Keys 3 and 7 swing an elongated red greave/sole toward screen-left, but the viewer cannot trace a consistent decorated limb through a clear load, release and pass. Labels alone cannot establish the anatomical sides.
- Keys 0 and 4 are both intended contacts. Their source alpha bottom changes y247→234; halo top changes y11→3. Keys 5/6 end at y231. The second row therefore appears lifted as a whole, rather than carrying the same body through a registered opposing step. No per-frame correction was applied in this audit.
- Shield heraldry/border, sword foreshortening, chest and belt details, cape contours and leg-panel proportions morph between related keys. The face and tall torso are more readable than the old proposal, but a higher-quality still cannot substitute for stable anatomy and cyclic movement.

Neither proposal shows clipping in its source alpha bounds (all lie inside 256×256); some old-source sword pixels are only one pixel from the left edge at key 7. Export timing/order is independently correct: all 16 variants finish at 3600ms normal or 7200ms half-speed, and every decoded normal-video frame chooses the expected source pose (235/235 per proposal, mean opaque RGB MAE ≈0.39 after lossy encoding). This does not verify pixel-perfect RGBA or game-world contact. Fixed-grid in-place playback does not prove heel/toe mechanics, material locking or physical travel.

The positive control is Raymond Schlitter's [Pixelblog 50](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2024/5/24/pixelblog-50-human-walk-cycle), visibly dated May 29, 2024. I decoded and inspected its eight photographed keys and eight color-dummy states. The former holds each key 140ms. The dummy GIF has 224 encoded 20ms frames; the fixed rightmost figure ROI has eight unique states, each held 140ms, repeated four times. These states visibly distinguish the supporting leg beneath the body from the passing leg, then reverse their roles in the other half-cycle. This is a casual side-view control, not an exact armored Captain carry reference, and its bob curve is not a universal recipe. Reference art is private analysis material, not copied into the candidate or claimed licensed.

Before interpolation, require a small color-coded whole-body drawing that visibly reverses support between keys 0 and 4, extends the supporting chain under the mass, swings the other limb through a readable pass, and keeps each leg's decoration attached to that same limb. Register the body and actual support locations in one fixed canvas; justify depth-dependent sole height explicitly. Keep the shield, sword, halo and heraldry coherent through poses. Only after those whole-motion checks should detailed costume painting and additional frames be considered. No target numeric rating is assigned.
