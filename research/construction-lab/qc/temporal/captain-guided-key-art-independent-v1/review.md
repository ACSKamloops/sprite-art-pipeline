## Blocking evidence

1. **No convincing opposite contact/extension.** In native/2x color and silhouettes, key0 has separated boots; keys3–5 cluster the boots under the torso, and key4 is especially narrow. The photographed reference has distinct broad opposite contacts at0/4 and passing/lift positions at2/6. Here key3 looks like the passing position, but key4 continues that narrow configuration; it does not complete the next planted stride. Perspective and heavy armor can shorten the apparent stride, but should still preserve alternating contact, support and swing. This reads as feet gathering and reopening rather than the body progressing over alternating legs.
2. **Load-bearing leg and heel/toe mechanics remain unclear.** The shield-side boot lifts slightly in1–2; the adorned screen-left leg lifts in5–6. Those are useful proposals, but the intervening3–5 poses largely retain flat, side-by-side boot silhouettes rather than a readable back-leg push-off and advancing heel contact. The thigh/hip portions are heavily covered, so the knee, sole angle and support silhouette must do more explanatory work. The ornate upper mass remains in nearly the same combat-guard configuration while the lower body compresses beneath it. Small upper motion is appropriate with heavy equipment; large unarmed arm swing is not required. The failure is the missing readable load transfer under that mass.
3. **Held drawing moves across the world grid.** Actual decoded0→136ms keeps key0 while the draw origin changes416,80→409,83. Actual600→748ms keeps key4 while the origin changes383,94→375,97. Both boots therefore move with the frozen whole drawing during each interval. The accompanying within-held-key sheet shows this against a fixed grid. These are7/8 horizontal native pixels and3 vertical pixels of whole-raster translation, not a boot-boundary contact measurement. If either flat sole is intended to bear weight during these holds, it visibly cannot remain stationary in the world. No support selection or contact compensation is supplied in the inspected report; measured plantar contact sliding is **not certified**. This consumption defect persists even if better keys are drawn.
4. **Lower-shell shape progression is insufficiently resolved.** The adorned knee's skull cup goes from a nearly circular front presentation in0–3 to a more oblique broad cup in4–6; the pale greave cuff changes width/relationship to the ankle as the boot folds beneath it. The lift is partly explained by rotation, but the sequence does not clearly communicate a stable articulated knee–shin–ankle volume. The shield-side boot changes from broad rounded toe in0 to narrower tipped-up1–2 and then returns to a broad overlapping toe3–5. These are drawing/volume continuity concerns visible at2x, not proof of changed limb length; hidden joints prevent confident bone-length claims. Stabilize armor shell construction with explicit anatomical landmarks rather than treating guide coordinates as proof of the raster anatomy.

## Identity and art limits

The source's head/cybernetic detail, halo, red/gold shoulder mass, sword guard, dark shield with gold skull/rays and hanging parchment/cape motif remain strongly recognizable. I see no wholesale palette change or lost shield motif. The original upright reference is an identity/still reference; it cannot establish gait. Upper-layer exactness establishes construction provenance, not anatomical volume or motion quality. The proposed lower art retains red armor and black/gold knee ornament but warrants explicit cuff/ankle and shell-perspective repair. I do not claim a quantified material-area, limb-length or contact-boundary audit.

The2x export reveals fine armor detail but also makes unclear shell progression and clustered feet more obvious. Native silhouette confirms that the gait issue survives removal of those decorative details. Half-speed decoded keys preserve the same failing order with doubled holds; they do not add missing motion phases. Normal-speed decoding supports the intended eight150ms source holds, not continuous-motion smoothness.
