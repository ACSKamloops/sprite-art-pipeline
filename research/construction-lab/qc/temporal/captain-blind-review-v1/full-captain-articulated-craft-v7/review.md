# Independent full Captain v7 review

The strongest remaining motion defect is the unequal readability of the two recovery steps. Cels 43–60 (0.717–1.000 s at 60 Hz), especially 46–56, hide the skull-side recovering leg behind the advancing flame greave and upper equipment. The silhouette becomes one thick leg column. In contrast, cels 10–20 expose two legs and cels 24–34 show a clear bent flame-side recovery. Colour provides small kneecap/boot cues in the hidden half, but the alternating release, passing and incoming support roles are hard to follow at game size. This is visible in the source, actual MP4s and world replay; it is not an export defect. See silhouette-p2/p3 and world-silhouette-p3/p4/p5. First repair should be a controlled fixed-camera/track/guard composition study that exposes the recovering boot and knee in this half, preserving grips and armour dimensions. Do not simply enlarge the stride or add generic arm swing.

The loaded upper body remains only weakly expressive. Across cels 12–20 and 45–53 the chest, sword and shield retain much of the same guard arrangement as the feet extend and return. There is small pose change, not complete immobilization, and constrained arm motion is appropriate for the equipment. Nevertheless, chest-to-pelvis settling, restrained counter-rotation and the transfer into each support leg do not make a strong heavy walking statement. Compared with the local photographed eight-pose walk, leg alternation is present but the readable support/release story is less complete. This is an observation about the visible performance, not a claim about forces or centre of mass. Tune the loaded torso and guard together after the obscured step is made readable.

The largest craft limitation is still the gap from the original master’s deliberate pixel treatment. At cels 0, 16, 33 and 49, red armour uses broad smooth specular gradients and rounded tubular side surfaces; gold shoulder motifs and the small face become soft bright marks at native256. Nearest 2x preserves those rendered samples, but does not create the master’s shaped dark gaps, controlled highlight clusters or crisp ornament hierarchy. The true384 render improves face and ornament resolution without resolving this style difference. The skull greave’s flared cream ankle cuff is especially broad and shelf-like at cels 12–20 and side view sample33. Preserve the articulated joint, then rebuild the surrounding ornamental cuff as an integrated rigid trim with clearer overlaps, rather than broad planar flaps. Side greaves and pauldrons need designed surface breaks and readable ornament placement, not additional indiscriminate detail.

The cape is recognizably cream with gold trim, and it stays attached in covered views. In the side/back samples 1, 33, 99 and 117 it reads as a broad trapezoidal curtain with soft vertical folds, a comparatively straight hem and considerable rear stand-off. The former back crescents are absent in these four back poses. Its secondary change across the main cycle is modest; richer fold hierarchy and a restrained delayed hem response would help explain its weight. Four side/back samples cannot establish full side/back animation clearance.

There are real improvements in the delivered character. The wider steel blade reads as a weapon with thickness; the oblique black shield and gold skull/scroll composition are much clearer and expose more of the belt. Correct handedness is retained, the blond head is more upright, feet maintain recognizable segmented boot volume, and the old calf strips do not break the covered silhouettes. This is dimensional articulated walking, with visible knee flexion and changing boot orientation, rather than a generic cutout slide. No obvious prop detachment or gross pose pop at the viewed actual wraps was found. The cyber-eye and facial identity remain less clear than the master at256. Halo placement is stable but frames the shoulder more than the face; this is a composition limitation, not evidence of a loose attachment.

## Independent judgment

Craft/identity: **7/10**. Motion/game readability: **6/10**. Overall delivered game sprite: **6.5/10, uncertainty approximately ±1 point**. These are fresh judgments of the whole visible candidate, not an average of previous scores or rewards for mechanical checks. It is ready for candid user visual review as a substantial prototype; it is not ready for production art approval or a 9/10 claim. The strongest improvement is the coherent weapon/shield and leg contour assembly. The strongest remaining repair is the second recovery silhouette, followed by loaded torso performance and source-style surface editing.

The world grid makes direction and travel easier to understand, but does not cure the hidden recovery or prove ground planting. No gross presentation clipping was observed within the selected fixed world crop. I have not approved foot contact, collision, force balance, finger grasp anatomy or unseen views. Reported numerical grip/ownership/GPU checks earn zero art points.

Viewed original upright nearest-512 master, local photographed eight-pose motion benchmark, all65 source RGBA phases at256 and384, every phase adjacency through chronological sheets, and selected v4/v7 poses0/16/49 as a bounded visual comparison only. Independently decoded all195 packets of each of the six actual delivered MP4s: normal1x and normal2x at60Hz, half-speed1x at30Hz, both native resolutions. Visually inspected packets0–67,127–132,191–194 of every file: full first cycle plus both actual loop boundaries and final tail. Later-loop interiors were decoded, not separately viewed. Actual dimensions/FPS/durations are in actual-video-probes.json. Native256 silhouette was independently encoded from source alpha, decoded and all65 phases viewed; enlarged61–64→0–3 wrap viewed. That silhouette is an instrumental reviewer derivative, not a producer export. Generated native384 instrumental silhouettes were not visually assessed.

Additionally independently decoded readable fixed crops (x500,y60,w530,h530, no frame following or per-pose fitting) from actual continuous world384 colour and silhouette MP4s. Viewed packets0,2,...128 and130,132,134,136: all65 first-cycle art phases and first wrap at120Hz. Odd within-hold packets and later world loops were not visually inspected. Root's tiny world overview first pages and Godot nearest2x screenshot at cel33 were also viewed; the Godot still provides no animation proof. World hashes/crop selection are in world-decode-receipt.json.

This interface provided still-image viewing of actual decoded chronological packets, not continuous timed video vision. Human realtime normal/slower playback evaluation remains open; cadence and seamlessness claims are therefore provisional. First-cycle duration is65/60=1.083333s; half-speed65/30=2.166667s. Source endpoint differs from cel0 by144 RGBA pixels, max channel10 at256;741 pixels,max22 at384. The encoded files repeat cel0, not the endpoint. I saw no obvious pose jump at those actual wraps; small shading changes remain possible.
