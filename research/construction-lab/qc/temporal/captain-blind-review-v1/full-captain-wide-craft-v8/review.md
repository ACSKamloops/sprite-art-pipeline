# Frozen V8 independent whole-character review

The second half still reads less clearly as a loaded walk than the first. The wider track exposes a useful far-boot cue, but does not consistently expose the far knee or the leg's passing role. The torso retains almost the same guard through the largest lower-body changes. Head identity, the plain black sole and the broad cream ankle collar remain substantial source-craft departures from the original master.

**Craft: 7/10. Motion/readability: 6.5/10. Overall game-art judgment: 6.5/10, approximately ±1 point.** These are fresh judgments of the assembled candidate, not an average of components or earlier scores. V8 is suitable for candid user visual review as a prototype; I would not approve it as the finished Captain walk. Real-time human playback remains necessary before a final cadence judgment.

## Ranked observed blockers and repairs

1. **Second-half recovery has only a weak boot cue, rather than a clearly readable leg action.** Cels46–54 (0.767–0.900s) leave the far knee buried behind the near greave/cloth mass. Cels55–64 (0.917–1.067s) show a small boot sliver beside the near leg, while the silhouette largely remains one leg column. This is improved over a completely lost foot, and should not be described as total disappearance throughout43–60. Compare the first-half forward skull boot at10–20 and the bent flame-leg recovery at24–34: the alternating roles are much easier to follow there. The fixed-world colour and silhouette pages reproduce the imbalance, so it is not caused by the transparent preview's missing floor. Repair the actual far-knee/recovery path or the fixed overlapping armour/cloth silhouette. Test a bounded anatomical trajectory change across43–60, preserving stance sole positions, rigid greave volume and hip/knee lengths. Further widening alone is not an established solution. Avoid per-pose concealment or camera fitting.

2. **The loaded upper body under-expresses the lower-body support changes.** Across15–18 and48–51, a boot reaches forward with a strongly exposed underside, while chest, shield and sword remain close to their retained guard. There is some body movement, but little clearly legible settling into support or coupled pelvis/chest response. The result resembles rigid guarded marching more than convincingly carried armour and equipment. The photographed walk benchmark supplies a concrete support/release/passing comparison; its free arm swing is not a requirement for this armed character. Repair with a small, coherent pelvis/chest load and release layer tied to the actual stance sequence, retaining both grips, sword guard and shield coverage. Assess the resulting mass story in the world consumer. These images do not establish forces, centre of mass or actual balance, and I am not diagnosing a physical fall from raster appearance.

3. **Boot and ankle surfaces break the master's deliberate material language.** The featureless black underside is especially conspicuous at15–18 (source31–37), including cel16/source33, and48–51 (source97–103). It reads as a broad black slipper patch beside much more detailed armour. The skull ankle's cream collar also projects as a broad light flare around8–20 instead of a compact layered trim. The red boot volumes themselves remain coherent; this is not a demand to flatten them into cutouts. Author an owned sole material edge/tread and a bounded collar profile/paint correction, preserving the actual sole contact surface, ankle articulation and source texture ownership. The lower camera makes these surfaces more exposed; presentation alone will not supply their missing craft.

4. **The head remains recognisable as blond but loses specific Captain identity.** At0,16 and49, the small temple/eye region reads as a soft grey patch, and the bright hair mass is a rounded dome rather than the master's compact, sharply clustered crest. The mechanical eye is present; it lacks the master's crisp rim/lens distinction at gameplay size. The source UV diagnosis provides a safe route: paint the owned front and side head islands consistently, preserve outside texels and verify several phases. A front-only atlas patch would miss the visible side identity. Paint cannot guarantee a missing geometric profile; separate that question from sharper UV contrast.

The larger source-style issue spans all phases: rounded glossy red gradients, softened shoulder ornament clusters and a broad cream curtain cape differ from the master's crisp dark separations and layered folds. The shield's black/gold design and blade are readable, but detail density is not equivalent to controlled pixel clusters. Material and outline authoring at native256 should be judged directly against the master before adding more ornament detail.

## What does work

This is recognisably an articulated armoured walk. Boots alternate, knees and ankles flex, and armour retains substantial volume. The wider foot placement does not look grossly bowlegged in the full pose sequence. That observation does not certify absence of a real-time waddle. Sword anatomical RIGHT and shield LEFT retain readable grips and guard placement. Halo and cape attachments remain stable in the inspected main view; I did not see the earlier visible cape/boot poke at cel22. This is a visible observation, not an independent all-surface collision audit. I saw no obvious large wrap pose pop. These strengths do not remove the blockers above.
