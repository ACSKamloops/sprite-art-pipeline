# Independent body-semantic-v1 diagnostic review

The strongest observable usability blocker is the second-half recovery becoming hidden behind the advancing flame-side leg. Frames 43–60 (717–1000 ms; captured source174–208) narrow into an almost single-leg silhouette, especially 46–56. Colour preserves the separate skull knee better than silhouette, but the recovering boot is difficult to follow. This is projection/overlap evidence, not proof of bone intersection or wrong captured gait. A fixed near-front elevated camera contributes; a camera/track comparison is more useful than simply increasing swing.

The main craft concern is the thigh–knee–greave transition during bend, especially flame-side recovery at 23–34 (383–567 ms; source134–156). The knee cap appears as a round button against a narrowing thigh and increasingly broad cuff/boot assembly; the shin surface and flame motif become streaked/less resolved near its bottom. The skull-side parchment cuff at 12–20 (200–333 ms) reads as a stiff folded band projecting over the advancing foot. Some contour changes are expected foreshortening, so I do not establish rubber stretching or broken topology solely from these stills. The uneven shell transitions and motif resolution are visible concerns that need a close deforming-mesh view. Boots now retain much better thickness and toe/sole mass than prior plate experiments; there is no comparable flat wedge collapse.

There is a genuine walking action: skull-side extension at 12–20, flame-side recovery at 23–34, reversed flame-side extension at 45–53, and skull-side recovery into the endpoint. Boots tip to reveal soles; knees bend around coherent thighs; the torso settles, shifts and rotates modestly rather than remaining a pasted upright portrait. Arms move independently, with a bent elbow and larger near-hand travel. It feels like a restrained, slightly stooped body walk, not a generic cutout bob. The powerful torso and large pauldrons retain mass, although the neck/head feel sunk into the shoulder enclosure in this camera. The arm action is acceptable as this unarmed diagnostic; it is not yet assessed for sword/shield carry.

The neutral A-pose reference has sharper gold/skull/wing/chain and face detail, clean concentric knee rims and broad boot soles. The rendered body preserves major red/black/gold ownership and skull/flame asymmetry, but shoulder ornament, face and parchment are noticeably softer/noisier at native 256. It reads as a small textured 3D render rather than the original crisp rich sprite finish. Nearest 2× enlarges those unresolved pixels; it does not add detail. This is a craft gap, not a reason to discard real volume. No equipment identity score is assigned: this candidate intentionally lacks halo, cape, shield and sword.

The official CMU07 preview shows a real skeletal walk, but its six supplied preview states are neither a complete same-phase loop nor evidence that this thick skinned body plants. The source contract maps each output i to capture88+2i, with cyclic channel conditioning; the output is not exact raw mocap. Photographed Man_walk_1_4.gif provides complementary readable extension/release/passing roles, not frame-for-frame phase matching. This body has credible corresponding roles, with weaker second-half projected separation. The 10.732° maximum toe correction and C0-only closure are declared technical limits; they earn no quality points. I saw no large pose pop at 64→0 in decoded boundary packets, but continuous cadence and velocity seam perception remain uncertain.

**Still/model craft: 7/10 ±1. Body-walk animation usability: 6.5/10 ±1.** These are independent diagnostic judgements, not a finished Captain score. Worth user visual review as a useful body foundation, not production acceptance. Strongest improvement is coherent rounded whole-body volume through true extension and recovery. Strongest repair is projected passing readability plus knee/cuff ownership, followed by a physical moving-floor presentation before contact approval.

Contact is explicitly unresolved. Transparent in-place captures remove horizontal root travel and provide no world floor, shadows or collision evidence. Lower screen pixels and visible soles cannot establish loading, skid, penetration, heel strike or toe-off. The producer's separate fixed-mask sole audit is numerical diagnostic evidence, not visual planted-ground proof; this review does not convert its bounds into definite visible contact faults.
