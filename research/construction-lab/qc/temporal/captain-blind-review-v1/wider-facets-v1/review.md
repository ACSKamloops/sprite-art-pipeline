# Independent wider-facets-v1 review

The strongest remaining blocker is the prolonged loss of a readable passing leg. Frames 30–45 (500–750 ms), particularly 33–39 (550–650 ms), collapse the boots and shins into one narrow column under the tabard. The preceding approach at 24–30 (400–500 ms) already coalesces the feet. This appears in all-source sheets and the actual moving colour and silhouette packets. A viewer can infer alternating steps over the cycle, but cannot confidently follow the supporting foot versus the recovering foot through this interval. Wider track in the model does not by itself solve the projected image.

The second blocker is lower-armour volume and its transition into the feet. At 0–18 (0–300 ms), the skull greave has a flared cream cuff with a thin plate-like edge above a small boot. At 36–54 (600–900 ms), the ornament becomes an oblique ellipse protruding from a narrow red shaft; the cuff and face foreshorten into an awkward wedge. These are visible shape judgements, not a rejection of 2.5D construction or unseen backs. The reference master has a more convincing rounded shell, broad lower-leg mass and substantial segmented feet. The new surfaces reduce the previous plain rectangular side slab appreciably, but the cuff/ankle/boot assembly still looks less dimensional than the rich upper body. I do not establish a detached joint from these images.

The guarded upper body does have a small lean and settle, most apparent over 0–18; the Captain is no longer entirely upright and immobile. However, head, chest, sword and shield largely move as one unit after this adjustment. The loaded transfer feels restrained compared with the travel of the legs. Equipment reasonably suppresses free arm swing, but should still produce a clearer body-over-support progression. Cape and tabard movement adds life without resolving the support ambiguity. This is a walk with recognizable recovery and extension, not yet a convincing heavy, encumbered walk at game quality.

The photographed eight-frame benchmark (Man_walk_1_4.gif, 1120 ms) provides visible contact, release, passing and incoming-foot roles. Its unarmed swing is not a requirement here. Its useful distinction is the recovering leg's separate path around a supporting leg and the body's progression across that support. The Captain's fixed three-quarter camera, shield and cloth make this harder; camera/track geometry and authored overlap may both contribute. These are repair hypotheses, not diagnoses established by image inspection alone. The most useful next repair is to make frames 24–45 leg roles unmistakable in the actual projection, then preserve the rounded cuff and boot mass along that path.

Identity remains strong: blond face/cyber eye, red armour, halo, sword hand, shield hand and cream cape remain recognizable. Gold motifs and red material retain continuity; the revised relief and curved edges are a genuine craft improvement over combined-v1's slabs. At native size the ornate upper remains considerably more resolved than the moving lower parts. I saw no obvious pose discontinuity at 71→0 or subsequent loop boundaries. The report records zero clipped samples for all eight exports. Neither fact earns quality points; the motion faults are present in the source and are not caused by export clipping.

**Overall game-art animation score: 6.5/10, uncertainty about ±1.** The surface improvement is real, but does not materially remove the preceding combined candidate's principal motion blocker. Ready for candid user visual review as a comparison/prototype; not a finished walk approval.

Coverage: all 72 source poses, chronological actual moving native-normal colour, 2×-normal colour and 2× half-speed silhouette packets, every first-loop source-pose transition and all three loop boundaries/endpoints. All eight MP4s were decoded consecutively; the other five variants were not individually viewed. The 2× stage packets were reduced with nearest sampling to fit inspection sheets, so fine 2× artefacts may be under-observed. I inspected decoded still packets in chronological sheets, not continuously ingested video or live playback. Later loops were checked at boundaries, not every repeated pose. This limits conclusions about perceived cadence and continuous optical flow.
