# Independent equipped-carry, camera and world-context review

Equipped-carry-v1's main blocker is a guarded upper assembly that behaves almost as one chest-mounted unit while the lower body steps. Sword angle and shield attitude scarcely change relative to the torso over the 65 poses. This is stable and recognizably guarded, but it communicates little equipment weight, elbow accommodation or load transfer. The shield covers chest centre, belt/pelvis and much of the flame-side knee, worsening gait readability; frames43–60 (717–1000ms), especially46–56, again become a near single-leg silhouette. Guarded equipment does not require unarmed arm swing. It does require a readable support progression and believable constrained accommodation, which are only partial here.

The sword is on the anatomical RIGHT/viewer-left arm and shield on anatomical LEFT/viewer-right. I saw no large drifting attachment or equipment pop through the cycle or64→0. Sword meets the right gauntlet near the crossguard and looks broadly carryable at native scale. However, the fingers/handle contact is too unresolved to establish an actual enclosing grip. On the shield side, a red gauntlet/hand remains visible beside the outer shield edge, while the palm/handle/strap connection behind the shield is hidden. That silhouette can look like an exposed dangling hand beside a shield. It is an ambiguity needing rear/side grip views, not a proven broken attachment. The producer's named-palm empty agreement tests a coordinate binding, not finger closure, physical grip or collision; no score points are assigned for it.

Bent sword elbow and broad pauldrons maintain a recognizable guarded arm shape. The arm/cuff regions remain bulky and visually compressed; the elevated near-front view and shield occlusion prevent a confident assessment of left elbow/shoulder clearance. I did not see a clear blade-through-face event or an obvious changing shield/body intersection, but do not give collision clearance. The blade is a very fine diagonal at256 and has limited weapon readability despite its stable outline. Shield ornament is rich but becomes an unresolved stripe texture when small. Compared with the unarmed render, ortho3.0 rather than2.45 makes the actor smaller in the same canvas, so some apparent loss of face/armour detail is presentation density, not a proven texture regression.

The actual legs preserve real extension and recovery, body settle and modest torso rotation from the body walk. They retain rounded shell and boot mass rather than behaving like rotated cutouts. The authored guard replaces free arms; it does not visibly recalibrate lower/spine/head motion for new equipment load. Cadence is therefore a restrained walking body carrying a rigid guard, not yet strongly convincing heavy sword-and-shield walking. No cape or halo is present by design; this is not final Captain identity/completeness approval.

**Equipped still/craft:7/10±1. Equipped animation usability:6.5/10±1.** Worth candid user comparison as an unapproved carry foundation. First repair should combine a clearer projected passing path with a visible, credible shield grip and some constrained upper-body load response. Technical grip agreement remains a separate receipt, not an art rating.

## Camera-only comparator

Full-minus45 materially improves the second half of the same unarmed gait. At43–58 (717–967ms), flame-side advance and the skull-side rear/recovering boot become separated, particularly45–54; the original view hid them almost on top of one another. Earlier0–7 also shows the bent recovering boot more clearly. This is useful camera evidence, not repaired rig geometry. It does not improve every phase:12–24 (200–400ms) and roughly30–38 (500–633ms) still compress the legs strongly in silhouette. Colour maintains more knee ownership than silhouette. The angle exposes greave side/cuff thickness and gives a more convincing sagittal path, while reducing face/chest frontal readability and looking more downward/stooped. It is a worthwhile camera direction to test with equipment; unarmed improvement cannot guarantee shield-equipped readability. No separate animation-quality points are awarded for camera metadata or for unchanged geometry.
