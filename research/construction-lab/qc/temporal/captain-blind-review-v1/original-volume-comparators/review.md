# Independent original-volume comparator review

Strongest source blockers: both actors lose much of the master's broad viewer-right flame greave and boot mass, leaving long thin lower legs beneath a very wide chest/shield. Longstride makes the gait clearer but at frames 30–45 (500–750 ms) both legs nearly merge into one narrow column while the upper load stays conspicuously upright. The resulting complete actor still reads as a moving armored display figure more than a heavy body taking and releasing weight.

Canonical cloth v1: **6/10**. Longstride cloth v1 source, presented in corrected longstride export v2: **6.5/10**. Uncertainty approximately ±1 point, particularly for cadence perceived during real playback. These are independent game-art judgments after inspecting actual exports, not scores for contact equations, source preservation, hashes, or successful rendering. Both are useful for user visual comparison; neither is ready to present as a finished approved walk.

## Exact observations and repair priorities

- At frame 0 (0 ms), canonical's foot separation is small and the viewer-right red greave is much narrower than the original master. At frames 18–36 (300–600 ms), the lifted/passing foot barely separates the two boot masses. The action is a restrained shuffle; equipment justifies restraint, but it does not explain away the weak change between support, passing and incoming poses.
- Longstride frames 0–18 (0–300 ms) and 54–63 (900–1050 ms) make incoming/recovering foot advance much easier to identify. This is a material improvement in walk readability. Frames 30–45 (500–750 ms), especially 33–39 (550–650 ms), then hide most of the second leg and bunch the visible knee, ankle and boots below the pelvis. The silhouette contracts far more than the upper mass changes. Occlusion during sagittal passing is plausible; the long duration and unusually thin support column are the art/readability problem, not proof that the modeled feet intersect.
- Both candidates' chest, face, shield and raised sword preserve identity and attachments well, but across the loading/release sequences their orientations change little relative to one another. Small vertical/body translation and cloth lag do not provide a strong load response. A constrained armed walk can keep the sword and shield guarded while still showing pelvis displacement over the loaded foot, a modest chest counterbalance and a perceptible settle/rise. Do that before adding more cloth amplitude.
- The original skull/cream greave details and segmented boot shapes are substantially better than volume-v3's smooth boot wedges and cell-like skins. The skull emblem now belongs to a recognizable plated leg, and ankle/toe segmentation gives the foot a clearer structure. However, matching the source pixels to a closed shell has not preserved the master's apparent armor thickness. The viewer-right red/flame greave in particular remains pipe-like, and its boot is small relative to the bulky round knee and shield. Restore silhouette volume and broad front/side facets through the swing, then review the f30–45 passing shell again.
- At native scale the face, gold trim, shield iconography and sword remain readable. Lower-leg motifs compress into busy narrow strips, more noticeably in longstride frames 45–60 (750–1000 ms). This is a source projection/shape issue; video compression can soften tiny details but cannot create the slim leg contour visible in the PNGs. The pixel treatment of the upper actor is richer and more coherent than the lower shell edges.
- Cape/tabard lag provides an observable secondary change, but much of the cape still reads as a broad sheet changing its hem placement. It neither repairs support nor supplies the missing equipment mass response. No conspicuous discontinuity was observed at source frame 71→0 (1183→1200 ms) or the inspected later exported cycle boundaries. This does not establish continuous-playback cadence quality.

## Benchmark and hypotheses

The concrete comparison was the verified eight-frame photographed walk `Man_walk_1_4.gif` in `/home/astra/artifacts/captain-motion-reference-v1`: contacts 0/4, release 1/5, passing 2/6 and incoming 3/7, each held 140 ms. Its changing support role and readable bent recovery knee were the benchmark, not free unarmed arm swing or identical stride length. The new actors do alternate roles and advance through the world, and longstride comes closer to that benchmark than canonical.

The source uses a fixed three-quarter view and a .58 stance model. A camera that projects sagittal travel strongly into depth can legitimately hide the far limb, but may need a slightly different authored spacing/camera or wider armor volumes to preserve game readability. The thin-shell projection and upper/lower coupling are hypotheses for the observed appearance; this audit has not measured equipment center of mass, proved anatomical instability or independently reconstructed the rig. The larger stride alone is not a gait repair.

## Actual coverage and export separation

All 72 source PNG poses of each candidate were visually inspected in three chronological native grids each, including all phase keys and the wrap. Actual consecutive frames were decoded from all eight variants of each v1 export (16 videos). Visually inspected packets covered every first-cycle source pose plus every cycle boundary/end for normal native color, normal 2× color and half-speed 2× silhouette of both candidates. The initial native-color review crop was fixed, not pose-fitted, but was too short for late longstride travel; those crop omissions are review presentation limits, not source defects. Whole-stage decoded color/silhouette grids supplied the additional coverage. The other decoded variants were not all individually visually inspected.

Longstride v1 has a real export-stage defect: its 2× normal variants report 218 clipped samples each and its half-speed variants 329 each. The whole-stage decoded late packets visibly cut off the lower actor. Canonical v1 reports zero clipped samples in all eight variants. Longstride v2 reports zero in all eight and has identical source-frame hashes to v1. Its actual 2× normal-color and half-speed-silhouette consecutive videos were decoded and their full chronological first cycles plus all three boundaries/end visually inspected. The corrected late packets retain the complete actor. This is an export repair and adds no art-quality points.

Tools delivered decoded chronological frame packets/contact sheets, not continuous video playback or ingestion. Normal and slower sequences were compared by their actual export timestamps; temporal perception remains less certain than for direct video playback. Receipts bind source, specs, reports, exports and retained evidence. No new source art was edited.
