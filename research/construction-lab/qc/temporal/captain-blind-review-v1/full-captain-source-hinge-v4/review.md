# Independent full Captain source-hinge v4 review

**Still craft 6/10; motion usability 5.5/10; overall game-ready impression 5.5/10, ±1 point.** This is a recognizable, dimensional Captain diagnostic with genuine limb progression, but conspicuous broken calf geometry and incomplete cloth/presentation prevent finished-game approval. Ready for candid user visual review with those limitations stated. The ratings are independently based on this frozen v4 packet, not carried over from v3 or awarded for preserved hashes/3D construction.

## Strongest observed blockers

1. **Repair the calf/ankle ownership and armour transition first.** At cels 0–3 (0–50 ms), thin red/dark triangular strips fan out from the recovering skull-side lower leg below the shield. They return around cels 58–64 (967–1067 ms) and across the repeated start. They are visible in the actual decoded exports, both source resolutions and the silhouette. This is a gross contour failure, not mere texture blur or a subtle preference about pixel style. The cream cuff also appears pulled across the moving ankle/greave at cels 5–9 and 15–20. The producer's known ownership investigation is consistent with these observations; I did not rerun the mesh strain audit on this new rig and do not substitute the old rig's exact measurements for this candidate. Require a closed, coherent calf/boot contour through all 65 phases before further finish work.
2. **The cape has a demonstrated visible penetration.** I inspected the actual source117 back diagnostic: two dark crescent openings interrupt the cream cloth near the lower hem. Source117 corresponds to exported cel58, about967 ms. This back view is additional diagnostic context, not the main camera or a full back-cycle review. The main view partly hides the intersection. The producer reports up to326 thickened-triangle overlap pairs; that count is technical context, while the crescents are direct visible failure. Repair cloth/body clearance on the authored geometry or motion, without hiding the affected pixels.
3. **The second recovery still loses its identity.** Cels43–60 (717–1000 ms), especially46–56 (767–933 ms), compress the two legs into one column below the shield. One red kneecap travels up the shield edge while the recovering leg is mostly concealed. Colour hints at the two sides, but silhouette readability is poor. Cels10–20 give a clear forward skull-side boot and cels24–34 a bent recovering flame-side boot; genuine extension/recovery exists, yet the two halves are unequally readable. Compared with the local photographed eight-phase walk, the release/passage relationship is hard to follow in the latter half. A camera or track change must be tested through the whole cycle after repairing anatomy, not accepted because one spread pose looks good.
4. **The upper body communicates a braced guard more strongly than a loaded walk.** Shoulder, sword, shield and torso remain closely locked throughout the sequence, with limited distinct load response. The head/chest lean down and forward compared with the upright master; the shield hides pelvis and belt transfer cues. Reduced arm swing is appropriate for gear, but that does not replace readable whole-body loading. I cannot certify which foot bears load without world-ground context. The upper-body pose could suit a cautious combat advance; it is less convincing as the requested upright encumbered locomotion.
5. **The cloth and several identity landmarks are still prototype finish.** The beige/taupe cape is a mostly straight trapezoid with a quiet hem and a few soft folds, unlike the master's richer cream cloth, dark fold hierarchy and finished trim. Its bounded lag gives small shape changes but little readable weight wave. The halo stays stable but sits high over the viewer-right shoulder in this camera, with a less integrated relation to the head than the master. The narrow near-vertical shield loses the master's broad oblique silhouette; crowded cream/gold motifs read as noisy stripes more than clean separated ornament. The sword is much thinner and more needle-like than the master's broad, shaded blade. These are seen design/presentation differences, not assertions about unseen canonical backs.

## What works and how resolution changes the result

Red/black/gold armour, blond head, skull and flame greaves, sword anatomical-right/viewer-left, shield anatomical-left/viewer-right, halo and cream cape make the identity recognizable. The model retains rounded torso, boot and plate volume over many phases; it is materially stronger than a flat rotating cutout at preserving depth. The sword/shield do not visibly drift away from their hands at the sampled phases or wraps. Finger-level grip and rear shield handle are not resolved enough to approve exact contact. Backpack visibility is too limited in the main view for a design verdict.

True384 resolves more face structure and a clearer shield skull than256, as personally seen in paired native nearest2x crops. It also resolves the calf defects more clearly. It does not repair silhouette, joint volume, cloth penetration, or loading. Native256 often loses facial expression and gold ornament into small bright clusters; increasing detail count alone does not reproduce the deliberate edges and value groups of the master. The 384 motion retains the same observed problems. No extra motion/art points are awarded for actual higher-resolution rendering.

## Controlled nine-panel shader study

I inspected all nine384 panels against the master. This study is on the previous integrated scene SHA102907336c0f624443a9863d8a531f77e387f641ce3c470b009b2b9fc098c25e, **not a full v4 shader animation**. Only body material changes, at source frames1/33/99; props keep their original lighting. Therefore unchanged cape/shield/sword appearance is not evidence that a body shader fixes them.

Unlit paint gives the face and red armour more readable illustration colours and reduces the dark/re-lit appearance. It buys a real local readability improvement rather than merely more pixels: the panels have the same384 resolution. At source33 the red boot and shin are brighter and easier to separate. The cost is flatter plate/boot curvature and weaker directional lighting, particularly the cheek/head and broad red shell faces. Paint-with-AO restores some local recess separation while retaining much of that colour benefit. **Paint-with-AO is the more promising next controlled trial**, but the difference from unlit is modest in these three poses, and none reaches the master's crisp, intentionally grouped finish. AO cannot be treated as foot/contact shadow proof. No full-cycle shader choice or animation score improvement is approved from three stills. Repair geometry first; then compare the AO treatment across all phases at the intended native256 game scale.
