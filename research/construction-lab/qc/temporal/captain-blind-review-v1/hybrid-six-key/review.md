# Independent hybrid six-key source-art trial review

**Promising improvement in local leg drawing; still needs revision as a game walk. Integrated visual quality: 5.5/10, uncertainty +/-1. Not ready for finished-walk user approval. It is suitable only for explicitly labeled technique comparison.** No credit is awarded for hash equality or exact upper-part reuse.

## Observable defects, before score rationale

- **Supporting feet do not maintain a convincing consistent support plane.** For the first half, the skull boot's bottom changes from native y242 at key0/0ms to y244 at key1/200ms to y239 at key2/400ms. In the opposite half, the plain boot bottom is approximately y242 at key3/600ms, y244 at key4/800ms and y241 at key5/1000ms. These are visible fixed-canvas sole changes, not proof of game-engine penetration: the audition has no physical floor/contact test. Fore/aft movement of a stance boot can be valid for an in-place treadmill walk; the vertical changes are the more significant concern. The upper torso lowers while the supposed loaded sole lowers too, rather than demonstrating a planted foot and compression above it. Passing should lift the other boot, not shift the supporting baseline.

- **Transitions change mass and extension too abruptly.** Key1->2 at 200->400ms takes the plain boot from a small lifted shape into a much larger, sole-facing incoming boot with no convincing progressive extension between them. Key2->3 at 400->600ms switches the apparent support side while the larger incoming mass changes again. Key4->5 at 800->1000ms has the corresponding skull-boot presentation change. Some change in apparent size is expected from foreshortening; this candidate does not connect those shapes smoothly enough to distinguish perspective from changing anatomy. The skull kneecap surround, cuff and boot masses are less stable than the reused upper body. Both boots are bulkier and rounder than the upright master, and the skull medallion has been enlarged/redrawn.

- **Whole-body loading remains disconnected from the leg proposals.** The torso/head/equipment trajectory is a small borrowed upright bob, while six lower-body illustrations change underneath it. The contact-to-release-to-incoming sequence is broadly more plausible than a repeated wide stride, but down/compression and rising support have not been convincingly authored in the whole silhouette. At key3->4, the far passing boot has little clear release/clearance; much of the visible change is leg spacing and the redraw of the skull plate. This needs phase-specific hip, knee and ankle organization, with the trunk responding to the supporting foot. Equipment supports restrained shoulders and arms; I do not require unarmed arm swing.

- **Cadence and wrap reveal the disconnected keys.** Six 200ms holds, doubled to 400ms in the slower exports, give the sequence conspicuous picture changes. Key5->0 at 1000->1200ms widens/separates the legs and changes the incoming skull sole into a planted boot presentation abruptly. The same change repeats at 2200->2400ms and 3400->3600ms; it is not an isolated export reset. Smoothing can help cadence only after the underlying support and body proportions are coherent.

## What improves materially over the v4 rotated-part stills

Yes: **pose-specific shells improve local volume and depth enough to justify this technique.** Key2/400ms and key5/1000ms show purposeful boot-sole perspective and incoming-foot presentation. The knees and greaves form rounded armor volumes rather than rotating a preserved frontal decoration into the earlier sharply splayed outside-knee cutout. The exposed flat dark-red wedge seen around the articulated v4 thigh is substantially less apparent. The change is visible in actual decoded color and silhouette exports, not inferred from the construction method.

This does not establish a finished gait or a generally superior animation. New shells trade rigid cutout motion for redraw consistency problems, oversized lower-leg masses and weak support registration. Keep the depth-specific shell approach, but author it to a common anatomy/motion guide instead of treating each drawing as a complete independent limb solution.

The exact upright upper source preserves recognizable head, halo, sword, shield and cape identity. I did not observe hand swapping, gear disappearance or major grip separation. Main upper material richness is stable. The generated lower part uses broader, smoother shading and altered decorations, so its pixel clusters and material detail do not fully match the established upper source. Small black seam/slit artifacts remain visible around the borrowed cape and shoulder overlaps, but gait and limb consistency are the stronger blockers.

## Strongest remaining repair

**Lock one coherent six-key skeleton and support trajectory, then repaint the drawn shells onto it.** Establish per-side sole/heel/toe planes, comparable joint lengths and boot dimensions, two recognizable support/loading halves, and a trunk trajectory matched to those halves. Preserve necessary perspective variation, but make the transitions geometrically explainable. Do not independently recenter/fit each generated image to hide support or proportion defects. Add an incoming-to-contact and loaded-to-release pose if six held keys cannot communicate the action.

## Evidence and limits

Primary benchmark remains the verified photographed eight-frame `Man_walk_1_4.gif`: clear support, trailing-foot release, passing clearance and incoming heel are the relevant comparison, adapted to heavy equipment. The approved upright `native-256.png` establishes identity/proportions. The new source native sheet and all six composites were inspected; the v4 source stills supply a direct visual technique comparison, not inherited quality approval.

All eight actual MP4 variants were decoded consecutively after `report.json` existed. Chronological grids inspect all six keys over all three loops, normal and slower, color and silhouette, 1x and 2x. An actual full exported 2x frame at 400ms was inspected separately. Decoded counts, exact source/video hashes and selected indexes are recorded beside this review. Shared crop normalization avoids per-pose fitting; 2x grids are reduced by nearest sampling, with the separate full-size exported frame retaining actual enlargement.

The interface supplies decoded stills and chronological sequences, **not continuous video ingestion**. The score's uncertainty reflects that limit. No locomotion controller, moving-root variant, support-plane runtime, interruption, or additional character direction was tested. The repeated defects already occur in source composites and are not attributable to video order/color encoding. Phase labels remain proposals, not evidence that named contact/passing roles were achieved.
