# Independent Captain motion review: frozen v4

**Decision: both versions need source motion revision. Eight keys: 4.5/10. Dense interpolation: 5/10. Uncertainty approximately +/-1 point. Neither is ready for user approval as a finished game walk; both can be shown only as explicitly unfinished construction trials.** These are integrated visual game-quality judgments, not scores derived from hashes, frame counts, rig mechanics or the quality of the still alone.

## Strongest blocking observations

1. **The stride does not convincingly progress from release through passing to a forward landing.** Eight key 2 at 300ms lifts the viewer-right boot diagonally inward; key 3 at 450ms mostly lowers that same boot without a strong incoming heel or extended leg silhouette. Keys 6/7 at 900/1050ms repeat the problem on the other side. In silhouette, the feet remain in much the same separated lanes and bob/lift rather than exchanging clear front/back roles. Dense frames 18-27 and 54-63 preserve this sequence; interpolation improves continuity but does not supply the missing extension. The moving-root presentation translates this pose action across the grid, which makes the source weakness easier to notice rather than repairing it. Encumbered equipment justifies small arm movement and shorter steps, but not the absence of readable forward step mechanics.

2. **The far leg reads as a rotating illustration segment rather than a limb changing depth.** The skull knee swings outward left through eight keys 5-7 (750-1050ms), strongest at key 6/900ms and dense frames 48-63/800-1050ms. The upper connection is a broad flat dark-red wedge while the knee/greave retains its decorative face and the boot retains nearly the original viewing angle. The silhouette develops a sharply splayed outside knee. This makes the swing look like an outward hinged cutout. Rigid armor is appropriate; preserving the same illustration-facing knee and boot throughout substantial bend is the problem. The lifted viewer-right leg at key 2 also keeps too much of its rest boot presentation.

3. **Weight and foot release are under-authored.** At contacts 0/600ms, the image remains close to a wide, guarded rest pose. Down keys 150/750ms show some trunk lowering, and the far/near leg does alternate lifting, so this is not a static still. But flat stance soles, minimal observable heel/toe roll, and little whole-body lateral response make support/load/release weak. The visually heavy shield, cape and halo mostly ride with the upright trunk; the shoulders remain unusually locked while the lower segments perform most of the action. A cautious armored walk can keep the sword and shield controlled; it still needs support-side pelvis/trunk organization and a convincing trailing-foot release. I am not requesting free unarmed arm swing.

4. **The eight-key timing exposes large pose switches; dense smoothing trades that for persistent mechanical motion.** Each eight pose is held 150ms (300ms in slower export). The prominent 1->2 and 5->6 leg lifts look like discrete jumps in position. Dense steps are less abrupt, and the final 71->0 wrap is small at 1183->1200ms, but the same geometric leg arcs remain visible. There is no special catastrophic loop reset; the ordinary eight-key 7->0 switch at 1050->1200ms is still a noticeable stance/boot change. The second-loop ending was checked too.

## Identity, materials, attachments and pixel craft

The red armor, blond cyber-eye head, gold halo, cream cape, viewer-left sword and viewer-right black/gold shield remain recognizably the approved upright Captain. Head size, sword grip, shield design and rich material texture are substantially more stable than the leg anatomy. I did not observe equipment disappearing or changing hand, or a major sword/grip disconnection. This is the strongest aspect of this candidate and should be preserved in revision.

Nearest enlargement retains readable pixel clusters, though rigid part rotation makes small contour/trim steps shimmer. Flat exposed red backing at the articulated thigh contrasts with the modeled decorative armor. Silhouette also exposes intermittent fine dark slits at the cape's inner boundary and shoulder/chest overlap (notably around dense 60-66, 1000-1100ms); this warrants source alpha/overlap cleanup, but is secondary to the gait. Some gaps are intentional separation around equipment, so I do not treat every dark pixel as a defect. The cape acts chiefly as a rigid swung mass, with little delayed hem response. Additional secondary cloth motion should follow the corrected walk rather than conceal it.

## Benchmark and method

Primary motion benchmark: the verified local 8-frame photographed `Man_walk_1_4.gif` (140ms per frame, 1120ms loop). Its contact 0/4, visible trailing-foot release 1/5, passing clearance 2/6 and incoming heel 3/7 clearly express an advancing gait. I compared support/phase readability, not the photographer's unarmed arm range or exact stride dimensions. Identity reference: the new `upright-master-v1/native-256.png`. The dummy reference was available but the photographed sequence was the concrete primary benchmark.

Actual MP4 files were decoded consecutively, preserving VFR packets with `-vsync 0`; the receipt lists decoded counts and selected frame indexes for all 32 supplied color/silhouette, normal/slower, 1x/2x, in-place/moving variants. Frame observations use the source export report's millisecond timestamps. Generated chronological contact grids keep a shared crop and never fit each pose separately. Native grids compare both eight-key loops including endpoints. Dense grids inspect every first-loop source pose and both wrap/end samples, with moving color and slower silhouette full phase coverage. An actual full 2x exported frame at source 900ms was inspected separately. Several redundant variants were decoded but not individually visually inspected; the retained viewed sheets identify actual visual coverage.

**Limit: this tool interface provided decoded still frames and chronological grids, not continuous video ingestion or human real-time playback.** The normal and half-speed timestamps/holds were checked, but perception of uninterrupted cadence therefore has lower confidence. That limit motivates score uncertainty and is not a reason to award approval. Runtime/interruption transitions, another viewing direction, an actual game-camera locomotion controller and GPU sole contact were not tested by this review.

The skill's generic quality-gate reference contained historical Captain examples and historical ratings; that incidental exposure was disclosed to root before seeing this candidate. No project prior review ratings or user-feedback files were opened. No prior result supplies this verdict.

## Source versus export

The primary blocking observations are already present in frozen PNG keys and remain in both in-place and physical-root exports. They require source anatomy/pose/timing work. I found no evidence that ordering, an encoder color shift or a missing export pose created them. Moving videos intentionally show source-pose holds during actor translation; any stance slip within those holds is a sampling/root-policy concern distinct from the missing authored stride. The video's grid is a coordinate aid, not a rendered support plane, so this review does not claim measured floor penetration from it. Encoding reports permit tiny RGB differences; those are immaterial to this judgment.

## Most useful next revision

Author two strong contact/extension keys and two true release/passing keys around coherent three-quarter leg depth. Keep the main trunk/equipment identity, but let pelvis/trunk loading respond to the actual supporting foot. Repair hidden thigh and cuff volume for those keys before interpolation; make incoming heel and trailing toe unambiguous. Then inspect the full moving loop again. Adding frames or simply increasing root travel will not by itself fix the current articulated cutout appearance.
