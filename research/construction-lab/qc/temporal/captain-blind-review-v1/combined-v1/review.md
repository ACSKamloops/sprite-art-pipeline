# Independent combined-v1 full-actor review

The strongest blocking art fault is the broad, nearly straight-sided red extrusion beside the skull/cream greave. It is conspicuous at frames 0–18 (0–300 ms), then again 60–71 (1000–1183 ms). It restores thickness but reads as a plain rectangular panel beside a richly finished curved face plate. The strongest motion fault remains the long contraction into a single-leg silhouette at frames 30–45 (500–750 ms), especially 33–39 (550–650 ms): a bulky loaded actor briefly reads like a rigid upper statue balanced on one bundled lower column.

**Game-art walk score: 6.5/10, uncertainty approximately ±1.** This is a useful, recognizable walk prototype for candid user visual review, not a finished walk approval. The judgment comes from source art and actual chronological exported frames, not successful shell closure, contact math, part hashes or unclipped export. Improvements in upper loading and lower width are observable, but the remaining craft/readability faults keep the complete result around the same quality band as the previous longstride source.

## What the complete sequence shows

Frames 0–9 (0–150 ms) show the planted skull-side leg and recovery of the opposite foot clearly. Frames 9–18 (150–300 ms) include a modest upper settle/lean, rather than the earlier essentially unchanged upper orientation. The shield and guarded sword remain attached and preserve their anatomical ownership. The loaded upper change is an improvement over original-volume longstride presented in v2, but small: chest/head/shield/halo still travel as a tightly coupled rigid arrangement. At the next side of support, frames 36–45 (600–750 ms), the upper body does not visibly redistribute enough of its large shield/chest mass to make the narrow silhouette feel grounded. More arm swing is not the prescription; a clearer pelvis-over-support shift and restrained chest/equipment counterbalance would be more useful.

Frames 18–30 (300–500 ms) progressively overlap the boot masses. Frames 30–45 (500–750 ms) sustain the merged lower silhouette. At 45–54 (750–900 ms) the skull-side knee and lifted boot finally separate again. This is much clearer walking than canonical's small shuffle, and the alternating recovery is recognizable. Legitimate three-quarter occlusion explains some overlap, but does not automatically make a quarter-cycle of nearly single-column readability good for a game sprite. Compare the support/recovery distinction in the photographed eight-frame benchmark, particularly passing frames 2/6 and incoming 3/7. Equipment warrants a constrained upper pose; it does not remove the need to distinguish support and recovery legs.

The wider flame and skull greaves now carry more apparent armor mass than the preceding longstride source. However, the silhouette and material handling are not yet the master's rounded power armor: the skull-side outer wall is blunt and parallel-sided, with little convincing facet/edge treatment, and its transition to the cream cuff looks extruded. At frames 45–60 (750–1000 ms), the skull emblem becomes an oblique oval while the adjacent red wall reads flatter than the rounded knee. Some perspective change is expected; the mismatch in dimensional cues is the fault. Red/flame motifs on the other shin are recognizable, but their side form remains less resolved than the shield and chest. Adding decorative noise alone would not solve this: taper and round the side silhouette and establish broad consistent front/side facets first.

The identity remains strong: blond face/cyber-eye, red armor, halo, cream cape, sword viewer-left and shield viewer-right. Upper gold/black/cream material contrast remains rich at native scale. Segmented boot toes and soles are more legible than the earlier smooth-wedge trial, but boots remain somewhat small under the now enlarged calf shells. No obvious detached joint gap, crossed equipment ownership or conspicuous frame-71→0 discontinuity was observed. Cloth secondary motion supplies a changing cape hem and hanging tabard, but is still subordinate to a fairly rigid broad sheet and does not explain body weight.

## Model and presentation limits

A fixed camera projecting much of sagittal travel into depth can naturally hide the recovery limb. Adjusting authored spacing/camera or the passing armor silhouettes may improve readability without falsifying support. The observed upper mass response is an aesthetic diagnosis, not a measured center-of-mass or stability proof. The primary human benchmark was `/home/astra/artifacts/captain-motion-reference-v1/Man_walk_1_4.gif`, eight verified photographed frames, 140 ms each; its support, release, passing and incoming roles were used, not its unarmed arm amplitude. Identity was compared with `pilot/space-marine-captain/upright-master-v1/native-256.png`; the previous combat image was not treated as the identity target.

All 72 source PNGs were visually inspected chronologically in three native grids, and the actual eight-key 2× source sheet was inspected for craft. All eight actual MP4 variants were decoded into consecutive frames. Visual review covered every first-cycle source-frame transition and all later cycle boundaries/end packets in normal native color, normal 2× color and half-speed 2× silhouette. Native color used a fixed 512×560 crop at (200,70), retaining later travel; 2× contact grids used whole-stage nearest reduction. No per-pose fit or stabilization was applied. Other decoded variants were not individually visually inspected. The report records zero clipped samples in all eight variants, and the visually inspected late packets retain the whole actor. No export-stage fault accounts for the listed source defects.

The tools supplied decoded chronological still packets/contact sheets, not continuous video ingestion or live playback. Normal and slower timestamps were inspected, but continuous cadence perception remains uncertain. Input and retained-evidence hashes are in `review-receipt.json`; actual decoded-video counts and exact inspected indexes are in `decoded-receipt.json`. No source art or other checkout was edited.
