Full equipment improves recognition, but does not make this a finished game walk. ## Observable defects and repair priority

1. **The body rig must be repaired before polishing accessories.** The cream skull-greave cuff changes shape through the cycle, notably cels 2/18/27 (33/300/450 ms at 60 Hz). This baseline is the old equipped rig, not the corrected contact candidate. My preceding independently inspected paired strain evidence tied this same body boundary to a 4.032 mm rest edge reaching 194.755 mm at cel 2, across shin/thigh ownership. That is structural context, not a new quantitative measurement on this integrated blend. The current raster still shows the compromised cuff/knee transition; new paint does not establish repaired ownership. Do not hide this by changing camera alone.
2. **Recovery becomes hard to read during cels 43–60 (717–1000 ms), particularly 46–56 (767–933 ms).** The advancing flame-side leg hides much of the skull-side recovery. The two legs become a thick column below the shield in colour and silhouette. There is genuine forward extension and bent-knee recovery elsewhere, so this is not merely a generic cutout sliding. Cels 12–20 and 23–34 provide more readable opposing roles. Equipment and cape add occluding mass. A repaired joint rig plus a camera/track test must retain anatomical coherence while separating both recoveries; the photographed walk reference makes alternating release and passage readable throughout.
3. **The guard reads as a rigid chest unit rather than a carried load.** Sword, shield and upper torso stay closely coordinated throughout the 65 poses. Encumbrance reasonably suppresses free arm swing, but little distinct shoulder/elbow/load response remains visible. The shield also blocks belt/pelvis cues needed to read support transfer. This is an authored-motion problem; re-encoding will not repair it.
4. **The cape and halo look unfinished against the detailed armour.** In the three-quarter cycle the halo is high and offset toward the viewer-right shoulder, with an unclear head/mount relationship compared with the original upright master's integrated halo silhouette. It stays attached perceptually; I do not diagnose an actual detached parent. The broad beige/taupe cape has straight sides, a plain hem and few readable folds. Its modest hem/flex changes do not develop a convincing visible weight wave. It reads as a quiet curtain panel rather than the richer cream cloth of the identity reference. No large cape jump was visible at the sampled wraps. Backpack depth/design is mostly occluded in these views and cannot be approved from them.
