The current V2 artwork is rejected. The user's criticism is justified: the character and motion remain visually weak across directions despite the correctly exported frames, stable attachments and repaired thrust guard. My previous `approved_prototype` decision was too permissive. It overvalued continuity and treated major anatomy, weight and directional-readability problems as minor polish. This new review supersedes that art acceptance; the original receipt stays unchanged as historical evidence.

This rejection binds final manifest `5543790d9fb8a3153a1c817d189fa16bb1e508b128a568b8e2c50508cca3be1a` and its six source hashes. No rig, generated art or historical report was modified.

I freshly viewed all seven actual-timed v6 chronological strips: walk, run, slash, thrust, shoot, sword knockdown and blaster recovery, each in right/left/front/back. They contain 112 timed pose cells across 28 directional sequences. I also viewed four original192×192 PNGs to check native-size readability. This is a fresh focused visual reassessment, not a claim that all96 clips or all6000 frames were re-viewed now; the earlier exhaustive adjacency inspection remains separate. I did not watch the continuous video.

The blocking visual problems are:

- **Anatomy and volume:** narrow parallel-outline limbs bend like tubes around a box torso. Tiny flat boots and a circular shoulder badge do not give the joints, armor or body believable mass. More seam stripes decorate the construction without fixing it.
- **Directional design:** the side profile has a projecting face and narrow torso; the front has a doll-like round face with dot eyes/cheeks and broad symmetrical arm framing. The back is a large flat hood/panel. Matching colors identify the costume, but the turnaround does not convincingly describe one volume from different views.
- **Mechanical gait:** walk alternates bent-leg shapes under a largely unchanged torso/pelvis. Front/back knees open and close sideways; run mainly repeats the arrangement faster. Contact targets can pass while the motion still lacks loaded stance, compression, push-off and appealing weight transfer.
- **Attack depth:** front thrust puts a long blade down the body, back thrust raises it beside the hood. Front/back shooting presents a vertical gun at the torso or head rather than clearly aiming toward/away from the viewer. Side attacks are clearer but still depend heavily on limb/blade rotations with modest hip/shoulder commitment. The fixed terminal guard resolves one seam, not the action's visual quality.
- **Flat fall/recovery:** depth views rotate the frontal/back drawing sideways like a card, then lift it back. The face, hood and torso do not convincingly redraw into a supported fallen mass. Ground-safe pixels and matching endpoints do not establish impact, collapse or a loaded push-up.
- **Motion appeal:** many actions retain the same torso and narrow linework while changing limb angles. They are orderly, but weak silhouettes, limited shape change and restrained rhythm remain. Adjacent consistency can prove that a weak design is consistently weak.

The next pilot should earn visual acceptance before expansion:

1. Author one strong native-size neutral sprite and a few deliberate key poses. Review silhouette, anatomy, material masses and identity at1x and2x before rigging or interpolation. Reject another tube-limb/box-torso construction even if its technical tests pass.
2. Build a small walk from explicit contact, down/compression, passing and up/push-off poses for both legs. In actual timed playback, show the support leg carrying mass, body response and a clean wrap. A faster oscillator is not a distinct run.
3. After one view succeeds, approve a real four-view turnaround and direction-specific contact/passing poses. New directions need authored shapes, depth and occlusion; one-view acceptance cannot be inherited as new-view art approval.
4. Add one attack with strong anticipation, strike and recovery silhouettes. Draw perspective-specific grip and weapon foreshortening. At1x the direction and action must read without labels or effects explaining them.
5. Keep source/image hashes, rejected versions, all key poses, adjacent differences and actual timed playback. Obtain a separate explicit visual judgment. Frame counts, IK reach, floor contact, exact GPU rendering and event correctness cannot satisfy the art gate.

The historical mechanical results remain useful engineering evidence. I am not inventing a new export, attachment or timing failure to justify rejection. The artwork itself fails the stronger visual standard, and a smaller genuinely authored-pose pilot is the appropriate next step.
