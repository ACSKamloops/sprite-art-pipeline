Blocking observations from the actual displayed states:

- Around 750–970ms (dense states 45–58), the screen-left gold-kneecap leg folds outward as a narrow decorated plate. Its thigh/hip connection is hidden by the hanging kit, while the visible knee and long shin turn almost together. The viewer gets a dangling greave and raised boot more readily than a thigh leading a bent knee through a pass. Around 300–450ms the other red greave is similarly read as a rigid panel being lifted. The source illustration's limb perspective is being rotated rather than convincingly redrawn for the new depth of the pose.
- The half-speed silhouettes expose a horizontal/diagonal background notch at the shin-to-boot transition, especially on the gold-kneecap side in the second half. The armor join looks cut through even though source alpha connectivity analysis of representative states finds one large connected component. I therefore do **not** claim a wholly detached boot; connectivity cannot establish a visually seated cuff. A four-times detail strip is supplemental inspection, not the game-size verdict. The producer separately acknowledged a too-narrow distal underlap in the preserved source split.
- The torso, head, shield and sword retain nearly the same carried arrangement while moving up and down over the hinged legs. A constrained sword/shield carry need not have free arm swing, but the support change still needs to read through the trunk and hips. Here the upper kit reads closer to a translated illustration than a body accepting and releasing weight. The moving-ground export adds travel; it does not by itself supply that physical impression.
- The eight-key version holds each pose 150ms and makes the exchanges distinctly stepped. The dense version fills the paths, but it keeps the same flat-leg construction and near-static carry. Interpolation is not the missing anatomical art.
