Scores are visual judgments out of 10. **Prefer B for Rowan and Lyra; prefer B narrowly for Brann.** These are choices of the more useful current walk, with repairs still warranted. Brann is the weakest preference: A has cleaner individual silhouettes, whereas B retains the recognizable orange rear sash and provides more lower-body progression. This is not an assertion that B is polished animation or that any variant passed gameplay acceptance.

All six variants remain recognizable characters. Both NE and NW read as rear diagonals. Rowan's orange hair, silver shoulders, blue tunic, cream trousers and brown boots; Lyra's silver hair, green outfit and teal scarf; and Brann's dark rounded hair, gold armor and blue outfit survive reduction. Brann B additionally retains the orange rear waist detail seen in the identity context; A largely loses that cue. The supplied cardinal art establishes these identities and turning relationships only.

## Visible findings

**Rowan A:** Good painted finish and stable silhouette, with useful arm/shoulder pose changes. The dominant long cream-cuff/brown-boot diagonal remains visually much the same throughout both cycles; the secondary boot retreats into overlap in 02/06 without producing an equally clear opposite stride. This can read as a held stride being rocked rather than a convincing alternating walk. I followed the visible cuff/boot path, rather than treating whichever boot occupies the screen left as a particular anatomical leg. Occlusion itself is acceptable; the weakness is the limited readable progression over the entire cycle. At 84 px the repeated extended boot dominates more strongly. The 07→00 transition is visually quiet; it does not cure the lack of a distinct second half-step.

**Rowan B:** More distinct wide, narrow, trailing-sole and opposing extended poses make a walk easier to infer. That improvement survives 84 px. However, the cream trouser shapes around the blue/gold hem become jagged and pinched, and the straight extended shin poses in 02/06 change into sharply crossed or merged shapes in 03–05. The transition cost is visible even on the light background; dark ground hides some hip details while the bright cream still flickers as a conspicuous shape change. The upper body has less counter-motion than the lower body. NE and NW have similar strengths and weaknesses. This is a legibility gain over A with a reduction in cel finish.

**Lyra A:** The best individual-cel finish in this set: coherent hair, scarf, sleeve metal and hem, with visible arm/scarf variation. The dominant extended boot/cuff remains in nearly the same diagonal role while the other foot makes a smaller excursion. Both halves therefore look too similar as a lower-body sequence. The contrast between animated arms and restrained legs is noticeable in the ordered strips. Silver hair provides excellent small-scale identity; on the dark background the trouser/boot grouping loses internal separation.

**Lyra B:** The alternation is the easiest to read of the three B actors because the green hem masks some of the abrupt upper-leg joins and the two extended poses are distinct. It still compresses to an almost single central boot shape in 03–05, with a sharp change from the straight shaft to the diagonal crossed silhouette. This can be a legitimate depth overlap, but the cuff path is insufficiently explicit at 84 px. It merits local repainting, not a rule that the feet must always be apart. The scarf and head stay pleasantly coherent, but the held upper-body silhouette makes the new legs feel somewhat independent. NE and NW are closely comparable.

**Brann A:** Strong rounded body, attractive gold armor and coherent cel rendering. The gait is still repetitive, but 03 gives a more visible change of boot attitude than Rowan/Lyra A, so I scored its lower-body readability slightly higher. At 84 px the dark hair/trousers/boots form large masses; the gold shoulder/forearm highlights carry identity. The missing orange rear waist detail weakens agreement with the context. The nearly repeated extended silhouette at several points still suggests rocking within one stride more than a fully articulated two-step cycle.

**Brann B:** Restoring the orange sash is a meaningful identity improvement. The sole views in 01/02 and 06 and the more central passing poses provide more progression than A. Several boots nevertheless merge into a compact brown block in 03–05; 06 exposes an emphatic sole and then 07 opens abruptly into the familiar diagonal extension. This is especially difficult on dark ground and at 84 px. The gray trouser/hem contour also produces a small outward projection around 06, making that pose look less clean. I do not call the overlapping feet impossible or infer an anatomical swap from screen position. The problem is that the cuff-to-sole path becomes difficult to follow through the sequence. This is the least convincing B motion improvement and needs the most careful local repair.

## Minimum meaningful repairs

If choosing the preferred B sets, preserve the existing character heads, torso design, palettes and directions. Concentrate effort below the hem.

- **Rowan B:** Repaint the cream trouser-to-hem joins and knees through 02–06 in both directions, removing the distracting tiny cream fringe/pointed joins. Make the same cuff's change from extended shaft to crossed pose traceable across 02→03→04 and 05→06→07. Keep genuine overlap; avoid creating a permanent artificial gap. A small opposing arm adjustment would help integrate the gait after the leg joins are coherent.
- **Lyra B:** Locally clarify the knee/cuff turn through 03–05 and the opening into 06. Preserve the useful long/short leg contrast and scarf silhouette. Small upper-body counter-motion would improve integration; the silver hair and overall identity need no redesign.
- **Brann B:** Redraw the compact 03–05 boot/cuff group as coherent overlapping volumes, then soften the visible contour jump from the prominent sole/hem pose at 06 into 07. Remove the small projecting gray edge around 06 if it cannot be made to read as part of the trouser form. Preserve the orange rear sash. Clarify one consistent light plane on moving cuffs so the boot paths remain readable on dark ground; do not brighten the whole costume.

If A is retained instead, the meaningful repair is a distinct opposing lower-body stride and passing poses while preserving its better cel finish. Cosmetic cleanup alone would not solve A's principal motion weakness. Brann A would also need the missing orange rear waist cue restored.

## Inspection and limits

Viewed actual diagnostic images through view_image at original image resolution: all 96 candidate cels at native 128, nearest-neighbor 2x and nearest-neighbor 84 px; each scale on light, dark and a diagnostic grid ground with the supplied pivot/baseline. Viewed all 96 ordered cyclic pairs, including each 07→00, on the diagnostic ground; the full strips supply the same transitions at the other scales/backgrounds. Viewed all three appearance strips and all 90 cardinal context cels for identity/turn context. Diagnostic backgrounds add no shadow, pose normalization or source correction.

These are **static visual and chronology judgments**. Normal-speed human-observed smoothness was not established. The timing metadata was not used as evidence of artistic quality. No engine, movement speed, trajectory, camera scale, shadows or collision surface was observed, so this review does not certify world contact, lack of sliding, or runtime behavior. The 84 px nearest proxy is a legibility check rather than proof of the game's renderer.
