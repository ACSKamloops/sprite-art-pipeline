Appearance and temporal ratings carry roughly ±0.5 uncertainty; motion roughly ±1.0. Plausible overall ranges overlap: A 7–8, B 6.5–7.5. Appearance/game preference is moderate confidence. Moving-animation superiority remains provisional.

**Appearance, materials and anatomy.** Both preserve the character's dark textured hair, face/beard, gold round pauldrons, navy clothing, orange waist accent and leather boots. A's gold highlight planes, navy thighs, cuff rims and toes separate more clearly at 84; its broader gradients retain roundness at 256. B retains attractive armor and facial focus, but its lower cloth becomes a flatter nearly black wedge near 03/04 and its boot shapes group together. The evidence supports a material/readability criticism, not a claim of an absent thigh, severed knee or extra foot. No visible limb detachment was established in either variant. Hands partly hidden by arms and feet hidden behind the foreground leg are legitimate occlusion.

**Temporal identity.** Head, hair mass, face, pauldrons, belt and costume remain recognizable over every pair and wrap. The bob, shoulder shifts, folds and waist-accent changes fit one character. I found no material identity replacement or severe scale pop. B's lower shadow changes reduce volume clarity but do not turn the actor into a different character. A's silhouette changes are larger in some pairs, yet the actor remains coherent.

**Motion readability.** A more clearly exposes an alternating reach, tuck, close-foot exchange, opposite-foot emergence and reopening. In B the same broad cycle can be reconstructed, but the 03/04 boot mass makes leg-role tracking less secure. Neither hidden knee trajectories nor planted support points can be certified from these views. Stronger silhouettes do not automatically prove more natural timing. A's near-similar 00/01 and 05/06 configurations could produce deliberate weight or a small hitch at the intervening larger changes; that is a playback hypothesis.

**Game use.** Both retain facing, identity and armor focus at 84 on all three supplied grounds. A holds cloth/boot distinction more reliably. On the dark ground both lose some dark hair/cloth outline contrast, especially B's lower body, but neither vanishes; gold, skin and boot highlights remain useful anchors. No distracting pale alpha halo, unexplained detached artifact or stage clipping was apparent. Retained RGBA softness and a broad palette are appropriate to the supplied style. The plain “game” ground does not prove performance on an actual busy map.

## Material issues and smallest useful experiments

**V1 — B lower-cloth volume, moderate visible limitation.** Inspect `B/southeast/03.png`, `04.png`, pairs **02→03, 03→04, 04→05**, approximately **x44–88, y80–114**; southwest counterparts approximately **x41–85, y80–114**. The nearly black upper-leg/hem wedges and dark cuffs suppress the navy volume visible in 02/05 and the identity context. The result is visible on light as well as dark/game grounds, so it is not solely background loss. Darkness itself is permitted; the issue is the reduced reading of cloth volume. Try just one alternate 03/04 pair with a restrained blue midtone plane or cuff boundary, keeping lighting direction, silhouette, occlusion and timing. Expected benefit: a clearer leg exchange and material read at 84. This is not a claim that hidden thighs are missing.

**V2 — B boot exchange, minor to moderate visible ambiguity.** Inspect **03→04** plus neighbors, southeast approximately **x45–88, y94–116**, southwest **x41–84, y94–116**. Similar brown toe/cuff values and contacting silhouettes group the feet into one mass. The overlap can be legitimate perspective; I do not require an air gap or infer a knee failure. Try a one- or two-native-pixel value boundary at an existing cuff/toe edge, or a small toe-angle adjustment on 03/04. Expected benefit: easier foreground/receding-foot discrimination while preserving occlusion.

**P1 — equal-hold rhythm, unresolved performance hypothesis.** In A, compare **00→01 vs 01→02** and **05→06 vs 06→07**, then **07→00**, focusing below **y81**. The small pose changes followed by larger leg-arrangement changes at equal 56.25 ms exposures may create a dwell-and-release rhythm. B also has a similar opening stance but more visibly changing lower-body arrangements. This is not a proven stutter: stills cannot establish whether the weighting is attractive in motion. The smallest next experiment is human blind normal-speed playback of the unchanged 450 ms loops. Only if a hitch is perceived, compare a small hold redistribution at unchanged total duration. Timing-only work earns no new drawing points. No further anatomy redraw is justified solely by hidden-joint speculation.
