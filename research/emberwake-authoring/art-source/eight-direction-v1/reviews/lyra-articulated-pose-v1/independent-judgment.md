## Bent-leg assessment

The proposal is not yet a convincing painted bent leg. A tucked lower leg can legitimately disappear behind the knee, and a lifted foot need not share a baseline with the other foot. Neither overlap nor the unequal foot heights is the defect here.

The enlarged joint proof shows why the form is ambiguous. The gray thigh/knee widens toward screen-right into a rounded, highlighted protrusion. The brown shaft occupies the left side of that mass, and the broad foot projects farther left. Their overlap gives pixel continuity, but the knee-to-calf-to-ankle axis is not expressed clearly enough by contour or shading. The boot cuff reads almost like a separate rim beside the gray patch. The connection is pinched compared with the bulk of the two adjoining forms.

The source04 and current06 near legs give a clearer progression from hip/trouser mass into a descending shaft and boot. Their gray and brown volumes have an understandable shared direction. The candidate exchanges that progression for a sideways grouping without supplying a comparably clear compressed calf, ankle turn, or cuff entry. A large new round gray highlight does not by itself establish knee anatomy.

The candidate's boot also reads as turning strongly toward screen-left while the torso and other boot retain their screen-right three-quarter orientation. Such rotation is possible, but this particular contour does not explain the turn: the upper shaft, ankle and broad toe do not read as successive parts of one foot in perspective. This is a local perspective ambiguity, not a requirement that both boots point identically.

## Material and scale

Leather brown, darker edging and gray cloth/armor shading fit the existing sprite palette. The replacement does not introduce a different character or a different rendering medium. The weakness is how those materials describe the joint: the gray-to-brown boundary reads more like overlapping cut parts than trousers entering a boot cuff.

Across the light, dark and game-background proofs, the anatomical ambiguity persists. Dark and game backgrounds reduce some edge contrast, but do not create it; it is already apparent on the light background. At 128 and 256 the narrow connection can be examined. At 84 the calf/ankle explanation largely collapses and the sideways boot remains the dominant new silhouette feature. The near-leg silhouette is therefore less immediately legible than either source04 or current06.

## Smallest meaningful repair

Repaint the localized near knee-to-boot assembly as a single bent volume while retaining the established upper body and far leg. Give the underside/back of the knee a clear route into a short compressed calf, then into a cuff and ankle with matching perspective. Move or rotate the boot as needed so the shaft enters that route; keep any intentional tuck and overlap.

**The selected geometry/protected-foot constraint should change if it prevents that local boot reorientation or repaint.** Preserving the present entire boot cutout and repairing only the seam would leave the strong sideways block and cramped cuff relationship in place. Permit changes to the boot's orientation, upper shaft and adjacent ankle contour; preserve its leather identity and characteristic toe treatment where they still fit. This does not require repainting the whole character or straightening the bent leg.
