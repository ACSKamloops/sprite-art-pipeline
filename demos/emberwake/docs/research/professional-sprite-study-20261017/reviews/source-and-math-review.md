This adversarial review evaluates whether the chosen examples are useful visual references and whether their measurements support the claims made. A commercial release, an open license, exact extraction, and correct arithmetic do not by themselves establish good art or animation. This is a reference-study suitability judgment, not approval of any Emberwake art. No desired score was supplied or assigned.

Scope and evidence

The reviewer read the sprite-animation-pipeline skill and its adjacent-frame-review guidance. Source lanes were inspected read-only. Only this adversarial-review directory was written. Mana Seed imagery and its analysis were excluded. No original character generation, sprite edits, local models, project/canonical asset changes, or publication occurred.

Native-size ordered source images and fixed-canvas adjacent pairs were inspected. Every adjacent pair and last-to-first boundary was reviewed for both LPC selections, Tiny Swords Run/Attack1, and Duelyst Run/Attack/Idle/Breathing. Annotated enlargements were used to inspect the claimed visible features. This image tool supplies stills; this reviewer did not watch continuous real-time playback. Ordered stills support pose structure, consistency, and seam observations, but do not establish the complete perceived cadence at source speed. No eight-direction coverage is inferred from the selected examples.

Tiny Swords frame witnesses

Run indices 0->1 raise the helmet/tunic ensemble while the lower silhouette changes; 1->2 descends toward the next pose. Indices 3->4 show the opposite beat with a wider equipment silhouette. Indices 4->5 and 5->0 return toward the lower pose. The plume changes shape coherently with the body action, and the grip/cuff remain visibly attached to the sword motif across all six poses. The native-size visor and shield read clearly against the body. These are useful examples of stable object design and a compact authored run, not merely attractive isolated keys.

The shield obscures the left-side lower silhouette at Run 1, and dark boot shapes merge with outline/tunic/shadow. The existing nulls and image-side contour labels are necessary. A continuous left/right anatomical foot trace or quantitative heel/toe study cannot be recovered from these poses. This is an observational limit of a useful reference, not proof of defective source anatomy.

Attack1 indices 0->1 raise the sword-gripping hand behind/above the helmet and give a readable windup. The 1->2 cut changes the hand position sharply and turns the body into a broad bright slash. Indices 2->3 preserve the low sword/hand position while most of the arc dissipates. That large 1->2 delta is part of the attack design; penalizing it merely for being large would prefer an evenly spaced motion over the authored strike. Index 3 still holds the blade toward image-left. Its 3->0 reset is a conspicuous change back to preparation, correctly treated as replay reset rather than a seamless loop or demonstrated recovery-to-idle. The source teaches anticipation/strike/aftermath; it does not demonstrate a complete combat state contract.

Duelyst frame witnesses

Run indices 0..7 retain a forward-pitched torso, a large low sword, recognizable orange hair/purple armor/gold gauntlets, and alternating long/bent leg shapes. The cape changes its exposed shape across the beats rather than staying pasted to one outline. Indices 0->1 lift the chest/head, 2->3 settle into a different lower silhouette, 4->5 rise into the other beat, and 7->0 restores the first stance without an isolated design switch in the viewed pair. The sword/grip relationship stays coherent. This is a useful equipped humanoid motion example, although the small foot silhouettes and cape/armor mean a persistent anatomical heel track is unavailable.

Attack indices 0..4 load the large sword overhead; 4->5 makes a broad committed sweep with a pronounced torso change. Indices 6..8 continue its follow-through, then 8..11 redirect into a second overhead load. Indices 11..14 preserve the raised attack silhouette while light/effect pixels develop. The 14->15 sweep drops the body into the finishing blow; 16..19 sustain and dissipate the painted effect. Indices 20..22 recover the sword/body toward the ready stance. These keys provide readable anticipation, force direction, follow-through, and recovery even at native scale. Large sweep deltas and later effect-only variation are intended structure, not an automatic failure of consistency. The sword and armor proportions are exaggerated but coherent; they are useful design choices rather than anatomical measurements to impose on another character.

The attack22->idle0 and idle10->breathing0 boundaries were also viewed in DUELYST-STATE-SEAMS-2X.png. The attack's final pose is close to the ready silhouette and the conditional state sequence returns through Idle. The exact static source reading is attack once, idle once, then repeated unselected Breathing when queues are empty and no interruption occurs. The 46-cel profile deliberately samples one breathing cycle and stops. It does not change native breathing into a one-shot state or prove the original game's live state scheduling.

Both positions are opaque white slash pixels, RGBA [252,252,252,255]; the lower body is covered by the baked sweep. Attack15's right-boot sample [66,81] is pale gold slash paint, RGBA [255,243,164,255]. These are not distinguishable boots. The alpha-on-paint test passes because the effect is opaque, demonstrating that foreground occupancy cannot validate semantic landmark identity. The issue is a measurement annotation defect and does not reduce the source animation's value as an art reference.

Mathematical audit

The conditional stance fit has the expected least-squares solution. For normalized direction d, phase phi, and point p, it minimizes variation of p + L*phi*d and yields L = -cov(phi, p dot d)/var(phi). Perpendicular point variation remains in the residual. Signed negative fits are retained, which is preferable to silently clamping an inconsistent direction/contact hypothesis. The three supplied analytic unit tests passed.

MATH-ADVERSARIAL-COUNTEREXAMPLES.json supplies an explicit limitation: points (10,30),(9,30),(8,30) at phases 0,.1,.2 fit a 10-pixel-per-cycle root with approximately zero boundary residual. A different imposed root of 30 pixels/cycle makes those same image points move through world positions (10,30),(12,30),(14,30). Nothing in the sprite images chooses the fitted root. Even under the fitted root, holding a pose for .1 cycle causes one pixel of world drift between pose boundaries. Zero onset residual therefore proves neither physical foot locking nor correct runtime speed.

An initial code review found confidence/uncertainty propagation inadequate for interpreting these point estimates. The root author subsequently retained source annotations and uncertainty, labeled equal-weight fits as point estimates rather than probability models, and made contact fitting explicitly opt-in. The final real-source adapters set every track contact_fit_eligible=false and provide no stance hypotheses. This resolves the practical risk of giving uncertain contour/foot points an apparently precise contact result. Independent final v2 checks cover all 11 common clips and 142 exposure entries: exact source image hashes/holds/annotations, preserved unknowns, zero contact fits, and independently calculated phases, extents, displacements, normalization, and onset secants. The own-prototype entries were checked for data mapping/arithmetic only; their art quality was not reviewed by this reviewer.

Sample confidence is qualitative, including numeric LPC labels; it is not a calibrated probability. The 1-2 pixel manual uncertainty in Tiny Swords, point-specific LPC bounds, and roughly 2-3 pixel Duelyst proxy uncertainty limit precision. A visor junction is a painted helmet feature; a tunic patch is not an anatomical pelvis; a foot-patch center is not a verified sole contact. Normalization by one fixed declared body-reference height is mathematically consistent, but different character proportions/cameras make normalized amplitudes descriptive comparisons, not transferable target values. Duelyst's fixed 43-pixel denominator is the root analyst's declared neutral visible-body reference (head/hair top edge y41 to boot bottom edge y84, sword hilt excluded), not an author-provided anatomical stature. Full-frame alpha bounds and RGBA changes include equipment and baked effects.

Remaining acceptance limits

The reviewer has not viewed continuous playback, run the original games, verified action interruption/ground collision, tested the root viewer/browser, or accepted the Emberwake sprites. No reviewed source supplies a proof of world contact. Duelyst and the two finished Tiny Swords clips pass this scoped visual-reference suitability review; LPC supplies supplementary geometry. This suitability finding rests on native-scale pose readability, coherent rendering/equipment, and the complete ordered still/pair review, not source credentials or mathematical scores. The final v2 descriptive measurements pass the bounded source/arithmetic/unknown-propagation audit. No unresolved blocking issue remains within that scope.
