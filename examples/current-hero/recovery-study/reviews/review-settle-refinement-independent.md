## Critical 07→08→09

**A:** The hair crown, eye line, scarf, shoulder and belt descend/shift toward 09 rather than remaining frozen. This is a small but real native-size silhouette/attitude benefit: the tall forward-loaded 07 becomes a lower, more relaxed 08 before the upright idle. Boots retain 07 exactly below the edited join (rows 53 onward), so their planted shapes do not take a new intermediate wobble. The cost is in the knees: the screen-right trouser mass becomes a rather long diagonal, ending in a thin ledge over the cuff, while the screen-left knee rounds off sharply under the hand. The torso/hip is settling over virtually unchanged shins; this reads as a knee adjustment but does not fully explain the pelvis-to-knee articulation. A08→09 still changes trouser masses, hand placement, feet and rendering vocabulary abruptly. A is the best compromise here, not a clean anatomical solution.

**B:** The head/torso change is identical to A above row 51, so it gets no additional upper-body credit. A08 versus B08 differs only in rows 51–71, the knee/cuff/boot region. B makes the lower legs more vertical and carries the feet somewhat toward the exit. This helps the drawn connection of the screen-right knee to the shin. However, both brown cuff bands broaden/change contour together; the screen-left cuff becomes a prominent lateral bar, and the toe/sole clusters shift along with the leg. At native size the brown bands briefly become stronger accents, and the boot masses change instead of quietly bearing weight. These are actual outline/fill cluster changes, not mere internal shading. B also compresses the dark trouser section above the cuffs, giving a slightly shorter, heavier-legged 08 before another reshape at 09. The visual improvement in leg alignment does not clearly outweigh that new volume pulse. It is close to A, and a viewer prioritizing the knee connection could reasonably reverse them.

**C:** 07→08 is an exact hold. That is not intrinsically bad: it preserves all anatomy and support, and no penalty is assigned merely for repetition. The disadvantage is the visual relation to 09: head, scarf/torso, hands, knees, cuffs and idle posture all resolve on one transition after the held forward-loaded pose. In ordered stills it offers the weakest explanation of the last settle. Its leg volumes are no worse than 07, but its exit remains more abrupt than A/B. It can be preferable to a badly drawn in-between; neither A nor B is so poor that I prefer that trade here.

## Full-action strengths and remaining faults

The brown hair/red scarf/teal tunic/brown gloves and boots preserve character recognition through floor, seated, crouched and standing silhouettes. The movement is legible without effects. The supporting arm is visible through the seated and crouched phases; the rising silhouette is clear at 05→06. Dark separation generally survives the dark background, though trousers/gloves merge in the crouch. The floor pose gives a readable low entry and the final pose clearly reconnects with the appearance design.

1. **00→01 changes the torso/neck relationship more than the support drawing explains.** 00 has the head near the floor with a long exposed horizontal body. 01 rapidly establishes a tall chest and near elbow support; the hand/forearm contacts are small and the shoulder-to-elbow leverage is not explicit. This is a readable push-up event, but not yet a convincing loaded arm.
2. **01→02 folds the boots and both dark legs into a compact bundle.** The crossing/order is hard to follow; dark knee masses blend with tunic edges. The head remains clearer than the pelvis, so the viewer recognizes the person while losing the precise leg mechanics.
3. **02→03 gains a cleaner raised knee, but the rear leg's path is obscured.** The grounded hand is useful. The hip and crotch need a more stable dark division to show which thigh is carrying weight.
4. **03→04 makes the biggest support rearrangement.** The figure changes from one extended/raised leg into a squat with a hand still at the floor. The boots look placed for a crouch, but the exact tuck/plant and transfer of weight between hand and feet are underspecified. This is an action clarity issue shared by all three candidates.
5. **04→05 is a small forward/upper-body adjustment with almost the same squat.** This can sell preparation, but the visible shoulder/forearm change is stronger than the corresponding weight shift. The forward glove/arm is a compact tan/brown lump against the scarf and knee rather than a clearly separated wrist/elbow.
6. **05→06 removes hand support and raises the hips substantially.** The body reads as getting up; however the planting boot and knee extension are not as clearly staged as the head rise. The near arm sweeps behind, and its hand becomes a small tapered brown cluster, with less consistent glove volume than the final reference.
7. **06→07 lengthens the standing structure and relaxes the arms.** The head/torso become taller and less bent, but the knee blocks and cuff heights change enough to feel redrawn rather than solid limbs rotating. This is a larger whole-action limitation than the choice of 08.
8. **09 is visibly a different rendering treatment.** It has broader, flatter hair and garment clusters and simpler interior details than the shaded, more finely stepped action poses. The character identity is consistent, but the face/hair mass and glove-to-hand simplification at exit still create a small style/volume discontinuity. The appearance reference supports the exit design; the moving poses need to meet it more consistently.

Prioritize the shared hip/leg overlap in 01–04 and the foot-supported lift in 05–07 before adding further late exposures. For the late settle, A's stable boots are worth retaining, but its trouser-to-cuff joins need a clearer knee volume rather than another broad repaint. Playback is still required to determine whether the modest A/B advantage survives the 62.5 ms exposure.
