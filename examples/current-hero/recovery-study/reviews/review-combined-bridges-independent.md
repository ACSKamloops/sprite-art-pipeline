## Full-action strengths and weaknesses

The red scarf, teal shirt, brown hair, dark trousers, gauntlets and boots maintain an immediately recognizable hero. The low silhouette, supported sit, crouch and upright finish communicate getting up without labels. Native-size clusters generally remain legible on both backgrounds. Dark contours lose some separation on dark ground, especially where glove/trouser or boot/trouser meet; light ground reveals these joins more clearly.

The common action still has substantial construction gaps. 00's head and scarf form a broad flat ground-level mass, then 01 exposes a much more upright face abruptly. 01's extended legs shorten and gather sharply into 02; the overlapping boots in 02 make the far ankle hard to track. 03 opens the knee strongly and 04 compresses the body into a compact squat: this is readable pose staging, but the hip/knee relationship changes more than the small number of drawings can explain. The right-facing boot retains a long heavy toe while the other leg changes silhouette more radically. 06 releases the support hand and shifts the torso forward; 07 then raises the head substantially and changes the chest orientation. These are stronger posture jumps than either optional drawing fixes. The last pose is a readable neutral stance, but the turn of the head/chest and altered arm spacing remain a distinct final reset. Entry and exit are assessed from 00→01 and 08→09; no external predecessor or successor was supplied.

## Changed-slot decisions

**Slot05, B/D versus A/C:** modest help. At native size the chest/head have begun to rise while both boot positions remain stable. The screen-left supporting hand lifts, providing a recognizable release before 06 rather than holding the whole crouch and then jumping directly into the forward rise. At 2x the crouched legs remain cramped and the dark mass at the bent knee/hip does not gain anatomical clarity. The support hand is already off the ground while the body is still very low; this is plausible with both feet under the body, but a stronger weight transfer would be preferable. The hanging wrist/hand is slightly stubby and blocky. No new isolated head pixel cluster or extra boot mass is apparent. Net positive for posture continuity, small benefit for drawing quality. A/C's repeated 04 is a legitimate hold, with no penalty for duplication itself; the cost is the larger following change.

**Slot08, A/B versus C/D:** small head/torso continuity benefit, net slight harm to native shape. The intermediate head height splits the vertical settling change between 07 and09. Face and scarf remain recognizable, with no obvious detached head cluster. However, the screen-left knee/upper boot junction becomes a pinched, stepped dark notch with a conspicuous brown cuff intrusion; the lower-leg volume feels less stable than in07. The screen-right cuff also changes its edge emphasis before09. At native size these tiny shape changes read more like boot/cuff reassembly than an indispensable weight shift. At 2x the dark crotch and inner-leg void remain crowded rather than explaining a clean bend. This is not a new detached limb, but the negative-space cluster and changing cuff volume are real drawing costs. C/D's repeated07 keeps the clearer leg construction, at the cost of a single larger final head settle. The intermediate is not worthless, but I prefer the cleaner hold in this comparison. A/B's final reset remains visible despite it.
