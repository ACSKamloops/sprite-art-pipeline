# Frozen blind comparison: SE get-up A–D

**Ranking: A = D, then C, then B.** The A/D tie is deliberate: both improve the explicit support-release staging, and the foot differences between them do not establish a reliable winner at native scale. These are coherent, usable get-up candidates with a few readability limits. No candidate demonstrates a broken limb, missing necessary limb, or serious loss of the supplied character identity.

This is a cold review of the nominated anonymous packet. No mapping, implementation, producer report, prior score, baseline report, or parent-directory content was opened. No accidental unblinding occurred. No production asset was changed. This report and its associated files were frozen before any unblinding.

After the comparative assessment was drafted, the parent noted that external browser transport checks existed. Those results and the browser were not opened or used for scores; the note disclosed no candidate mapping and did not change the assessment.

## Scope and perception limits

- Inputs: the nominated `comparison-v2/packet/` only, including BRIEF, FREEZE, appearance, every numbered frame, all pose sheets, all pair sheets, and all four rational-hold manifests; plus the nominated blind-review card.
- Coverage: A–D, all 10 exposures each, all 9 forward adjacencies each, native 80 × 80 and nearest 2×, on light RGB (221, 217, 204) and dark RGB (38, 50, 60).
- Native pair sheets were derived by exact nearest downsampling of the supplied 2× sheets. Focus sheets merely re-present packet frames 01 and 05 at 2×. Neither changes the source drawings.
- The inspection was of frozen stills and reconstructed chronology with known holds. **I did not perceive human normal-speed continuous playback.** Terms such as jump, release, and settling below describe differences between successive drawings. Whether those differences become an objectionable hitch at normal speed remains a playback question.
- The action is a 1.2-second one-shot. The 09-to-00 replay reset is deliberately excluded as a seam defect. There is no supplied preceding fall or subsequent idle animation to certify. The final pose equals the supplied appearance reference.
- This review does not certify other directions, animation actions, equipment, collision/contact physics, controller feel, or integration.

## The two changed moments

**Opening 01 — A/C/D preferred to B for economical action staging.** A/C/D keep the knees gathered while the torso props upward. The boot shapes overlap, but the bent-knee volumes and neighbouring poses make the leg roles intelligible. B extends the lower body into a longer reclined silhouette, then gathers it again in 02. That is an observable out-and-back excursion across 00→01→02, not a missing-leg defect. Both variants have a believable supporting arm. B could suit a character who first recoils or stretches before sitting; the packet supplies no such performance brief, so the more direct version receives the readability preference. No criticism is based merely on B showing fewer separated boot silhouettes.

**Support-release 05 — A/D preferred to B/C for explicitly showing the release.** In B/C, the screen-left hand remains low and flat in a support pose, while the opposite arm is still folded across the front near the knee. The next exposure simultaneously raises the torso, draws that support arm back, and lowers the other arm. A/D spend 05 showing both hands released and spread lower for balance while the knees are still flexed. The load transfer therefore has a visible intermediate drawing. B/C can still represent a deliberate final push; the weakness is the concentration of several role changes into 05→06, not proof of impossible balance.

**A versus D at 05: no confident preference.** The head, torso, arms and upper squat read the same. D extends the lower silhouette farther down and gives the boots/shins a somewhat taller planted appearance; A is more compact. Both show plausible bent knees leading into the boots, a support base under the crouch, and a feasible continuation into 06. At native scale the difference is slight. D's lower boot placement alone is not evidence that A was sliding, and A's more compact stance alone is not evidence that D has stretched anatomy. I would not spend another production revision on choosing between them without human playback.

## Concrete issues and smallest useful experiments

1. **B/C, 04→05→06: late support-arm release staging; moderate readability weakness, not an anatomical failure.** In 05, inspect the screen-left forearm/hand and the opposite forearm across the front; then compare both with 06. The torso rises while the hand states change at the same boundary. The smallest useful drawing experiment is the already supplied A/D-style 05 with a lifted support hand and opened counterbalancing arm, keeping timing and surrounding frames fixed. Expected benefit: the viewer can see the hand stop bearing weight before the tall forward lean. Whether B/C actually feel abrupt needs continuous playback.

2. **B, 00→01→02: lower-body extension then refolding; mild action-economy concern.** The lower body lengthens into the rightward recline in 01 and folds back toward the torso in 02. That adds a secondary leg gesture while the torso is beginning its main rise. The smallest experiment is the A/C/D bent-knee 01 with all holds unchanged. Expected benefit: a clearer single intention to gather and stand. This is a performance preference supported by visible chronology, not a demonstrated joint or perspective error.

3. **All variants, 02→03: the tucked-leg route is compressed into one drawing change; mild common readability limit.** Seated 02 has readily separable bent-leg masses; 03 lifts the pelvis into the hand/foot-supported phase, with more of the tucked leg hidden by the diagonal tunic/trouser mass below the waist. The support chain is plausible, but tracing which lower-body volume passed under the hips requires the neighbours. This is most apparent at native scale on the dark background. First test a very small value/edge clarification at the tunic-to-trouser crossing in 03, preserving the outer silhouette and legitimate occlusion. Expected benefit: clearer hip-to-thigh ownership without inventing a gap between overlapping limbs. An extra pose is not yet justified by the still review.

4. **All variants, 06 held for 200 ms then 07–09 spanning 175 ms: playback hypothesis only.** The long held forward lean followed by short settling exposures may read as effort, or as a pause followed by a quick catch-up. The packet stills cannot decide. The smallest next experiment is human normal-speed comparison of the frozen top candidates on the intended background, with no drawing or timing changes at first. This is not scored as a proven timing defect.

I did not find a substantiated alpha fringe, canvas clipping, major material-volume collapse, broken joint, or wrong character between the supplied backgrounds and scales. The scarf, teal garment, brown hair, dark trousers, boots, and arm protection remain recognisable. The slight difficulty resolving the compact hip/leg crossing is a readability issue; it does not make hidden toes or overlapping boots erroneous.
